Author: Prof. Johnson Agharese Egonmwan

FRONT MATTER

Dedication

This book is dedicated to all children who deserve to grow up healthy, happy, confident and prepared to make responsible choices throughout their lives.

Preface

Healthy living is one of the most important foundations of human development. The habits children develop during their early years can influence their physical health, emotional well-being, social relationships and future productivity.

Unfortunately, many children do not receive sufficient guidance on nutrition, personal hygiene, physical activity, preventive healthcare, emotional well-being and adequate rest.

This book has therefore been written to introduce children to the principles and practices of healthy living in an interesting, practical and understandable manner.

Through simple explanations, familiar examples, stories, activities and reflection questions, children will discover that good health is not achieved through occasional actions but through consistent daily habits.

The book also recognises the important responsibilities of parents, teachers, caregivers, healthcare professionals and communities in promoting children's well-being.

It is my hope that this book will encourage children to become responsible participants in protecting and improving their own health.

Prof. Johnson Agharese Egonmwan

A Note to Parents and Teachers

This book is designed for children, with guidance from parents, teachers and caregivers.

Adults should encourage children to practise the lessons without frightening or shaming them. Children who are sick, tired or experiencing emotional difficulties should be encouraged to seek appropriate adult and professional assistance.

The book is an educational resource and does not replace medical advice or treatment.

CHAPTER ONE

UNDERSTANDING HEALTHY LIVING

Introduction

What does it mean to be healthy?

Some children think that being healthy simply means not having a sickness. However, health means much more than that.

A healthy person takes care of the body, mind and relationships with other people.

Healthy living means making choices and developing habits that help us remain physically active, mentally alert, emotionally balanced and socially responsible.

1.1 The Meaning of Health

Health has several important dimensions:

Physical health: Having a body that is properly nourished, active and cared for.

Mental health: Being able to think, learn, understand and solve problems.

Emotional health: Understanding and managing feelings such as happiness, sadness, anger and fear.

Social well-being: Building healthy relationships and treating other people with kindness and respect.

These dimensions are connected. For example, a child who sleeps well and eats properly may find it easier to concentrate in school.

1.2 Why Healthy Living Matters

Healthy living helps children to:

- Grow and develop properly.
- Have energy for learning and playing.
- Build strong relationships.
- Reduce the risk of preventable illnesses.
- Develop confidence and self-discipline.
- Enjoy life and participate in community activities.

1.3 A Story: Ada Chooses Healthy Living

Ada often stayed awake late watching television. She sometimes skipped breakfast and preferred sweets to fruits.

One morning, she felt tired and found it difficult to concentrate in class.

Her teacher explained the importance of eating breakfast, drinking safe water, sleeping sufficiently and playing outdoors.

Ada decided to change her habits.

Within a few weeks, she developed a more regular routine. She began to feel better prepared for school and more energetic during playtime.

Ada discovered that small healthy choices could make a difference.

1.4 What Children Can Do

- Eat nutritious meals.
- Drink safe water.
- Exercise regularly.
- Keep their bodies and surroundings clean.
- Sleep sufficiently.
- Talk to trusted adults when worried.
- Avoid harmful substances and unsafe practices.

Think About It

1. What does being healthy mean?
2. Why is good health important to children?
3. Which healthy habit would you like to improve?

Remember: Good health begins with good choices.

Activity: Draw yourself practising three healthy habits.

New Words: Health, well-being, physical, emotional, mental, social.

My Healthy Living Promise: I will take good care of my body, mind and relationships.

CHAPTER TWO

EATING WELL: THE IMPORTANCE OF BALANCED NUTRITION

Introduction

Food gives our bodies the energy and nutrients needed to grow, learn, play and remain healthy.

But not all foods provide the same nutrients. We need different kinds of foods to maintain a balanced diet.

2.1 What Is Nutrition?

Nutrition is the process through which the body obtains and uses nutrients from food.

A balanced diet contains different foods in appropriate proportions to meet the body's needs.

2.2 The Main Food Groups

Carbohydrates: Provide energy. Examples include rice, yam, cassava, maize and potatoes.

Proteins: Help the body grow and repair tissues. Examples include beans, eggs, fish, meat, milk and groundnuts.

Fruits and vegetables: Supply vitamins, minerals and fibre. Examples include oranges, pawpaw, bananas, carrots, spinach and ugu.

Healthy fats: Provide energy and help the body use certain vitamins. Examples include moderate amounts of vegetable oils, avocado and nuts.

Water: Helps digestion, circulation and regulation of body temperature.

2.3 Eating a Variety of Foods

Children should eat different nutritious foods rather than depending on only one type of food.

A typical Nigerian healthy meal could include:

- Rice or yam.
- Beans, fish or chicken.
- Vegetable soup or cooked vegetables.
- A piece of fruit.
- Safe drinking water.

The appropriate quantity depends on the child's age, growth and activity.

2.4 Breakfast Is Important

Breakfast provides energy after the night's sleep.

Examples of nutritious breakfasts include:

- Pap with milk and an egg.
- Oatmeal with banana.
- Boiled yam with vegetable sauce.
- Bread with egg and fruit.

2.5 Food Safety

Children should:

- Wash their hands before eating.
- Wash fruits and vegetables properly.
- Eat freshly prepared or safely stored food.
- Avoid food contaminated by flies and dirt.
- Drink safe water.
- Avoid eating spoiled food.

2.6 Limiting Unhealthy Food Choices

Too many sugary drinks, sweets and highly processed snacks can contribute to poor health.

Children should enjoy such foods only occasionally and in moderation, while making nutritious foods their regular choices.

Think About It

1. Why does the body need food?
2. What foods provide protein?
3. Why should we drink water?
4. What makes a balanced meal?

Remember: Eat a variety of nutritious foods to help your body grow.

Activity: Draw a healthy Nigerian meal and identify its food groups.

New Words: Nutrition, carbohydrates, protein, vitamins, minerals, fibre.

My Healthy Living Promise: I will choose nutritious foods and drink safe water every day.

CHAPTER THREE

EXERCISE, PLAY AND PHYSICAL FITNESS

Introduction

Children need movement and physical activity to develop strong muscles, healthy bones and active minds.

Exercise does not always mean going to a gym. Running, dancing, cycling, swimming and playing games are all ways of keeping physically active.

3.1 What Is Physical Exercise?

Physical exercise refers to activities that make the body move and use energy.

Examples include:

- Walking.
- Running.
- Skipping.
- Dancing.
- Cycling.
- Swimming.
- Playing football.
- Playing basketball.

3.2 Benefits of Exercise

Regular physical activity:

1. Strengthens muscles and bones.
2. Supports heart and lung health.
3. Helps maintain healthy body development.
4. Improves coordination and balance.
5. Supports emotional well-being.
6. Encourages friendship and teamwork.
7. Helps children sleep better.

3.3 Playing Together

Children can enjoy exercise through group activities.

For example, football teaches teamwork, skipping develops coordination, and dancing improves movement and confidence.

Children should participate according to their abilities and use appropriate safety equipment.

3.4 Reducing Too Much Screen Time

Watching television, playing video games and using mobile devices can be entertaining.

However, children should avoid spending too much time sitting in front of screens.

They should take breaks, move around and enjoy outdoor activities.

3.5 Exercise and Safety

Before engaging in strenuous activities:

- Wear suitable clothing and footwear.
- Warm up gently.
- Drink safe water.
- Avoid dangerous playing areas.
- Stop when experiencing pain, dizziness or unusual breathlessness.
- Ask an adult for help when necessary.

Children should aim for an average of at least 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity daily, appropriate to their age and ability.

Think About It

1. What games do you enjoy?
2. Why is exercise important?
3. How can you encourage your friends to exercise?

Remember: An active body supports a healthy life.

Activity: Make a weekly exercise timetable with your parents or teacher.

New Words: Exercise, fitness, muscles, coordination, movement.

My Healthy Living Promise: I will make time for safe physical activity every day.

CHAPTER FOUR

PERSONAL HYGIENE AND CLEANLINESS

Introduction

Cleanliness is an important part of healthy living.

Personal hygiene means taking care of our bodies and keeping ourselves clean.

Good hygiene helps reduce the spread of germs and certain infections.

4.1 Washing Our Hands

Our hands touch many objects throughout the day.

Germs can enter our bodies when we touch our mouths, noses or eyes with contaminated hands.

Wash your hands with soap and clean running water:

- Before preparing or eating food.
- After using the toilet.
- After playing outdoors.
- After handling rubbish.
- After coughing, sneezing or blowing your nose.

Scrub all parts of your hands for at least 20 seconds before rinsing.

4.2 Bathing Regularly

Bathing removes sweat, dirt and germs from the body.

Children should:

- Bathe regularly.
- Use clean water and appropriate soap.
- Clean their skin gently.
- Dry their bodies with a clean towel.
- Wear clean clothes.

4.3 Caring for Our Teeth

Healthy teeth help us chew food and speak clearly.

Children should:

- Brush their teeth twice daily.
- Use fluoride toothpaste appropriate for their age.
- Brush gently.
- Clean between teeth where appropriate.
- Visit a dentist regularly.

4.4 Caring for Hair and Nails

Hair should be kept clean and properly maintained.

Fingernails should be trimmed regularly because dirt and germs can collect underneath them.

Children should avoid sharing personal items such as combs, toothbrushes and towels.

4.5 Keeping Our Environment Clean

Personal cleanliness should extend to the home, classroom and community.

We should:

- Dispose of rubbish properly.
- Keep toilets clean.
- Avoid stagnant water.
- Keep food covered.
- Maintain clean surroundings.
- Avoid indiscriminate littering.

Think About It

1. Why should we wash our hands before eating?
2. How often should we brush our teeth?
3. What can children do to keep their classrooms clean?

Remember: A clean body and clean environment help protect our health.

Activity: Draw six steps of proper handwashing.

New Words: Hygiene, cleanliness, germs, sanitation, environment.

My Healthy Living Promise: I will keep myself and my surroundings clean.

CHAPTER FIVE

PREVENTIVE HEALTHCARE: STAYING HEALTHY AND SAFE

Introduction

It is better to take sensible steps to prevent illness than to wait until we become sick before seeking help.

Preventive healthcare involves actions that protect people from diseases and help identify health problems early.

5.1 Visiting Healthcare Professionals

Children should receive appropriate healthcare through qualified health professionals.

Parents and caregivers should arrange routine health checks and seek medical assistance when children are unwell.

5.2 Immunisation

Vaccines help protect children against certain infectious diseases.

Parents should follow the recommended immunisation schedule provided by qualified healthcare professionals and relevant health authorities.

5.3 Safe Drinking Water

Unsafe water may contain germs that cause illness.

Children should drink water from safe sources and follow appropriate household water-treatment advice where necessary.

5.4 Food and Environmental Safety

Children should avoid:

- Eating spoiled food.
- Playing with dangerous chemicals.
- Touching electrical wires.
- Handling sharp objects without adult supervision.
- Drinking unknown substances.
- Playing near unsafe roads or open drains.

5.5 Medicines Are Not Sweets

Medicines should only be taken under the guidance of a responsible adult and qualified healthcare professional.

Children must never take medicines belonging to other people.

They should not use traditional preparations, herbal products or supplements without appropriate adult and professional guidance.

5.6 Malaria Prevention

Malaria is an important health concern in Nigeria.

Children and families can reduce mosquito bites by:

- Sleeping under appropriate insecticide-treated mosquito nets.
- Keeping surroundings free from mosquito breeding sites.
- Using approved mosquito-control measures.
- Seeking prompt medical assessment when malaria is suspected.

Think About It

1. Why are vaccinations important?
2. Why should children never take medicines without adult guidance?
3. How can families reduce mosquito bites?

Remember: Prevention, early attention and proper treatment protect health.

Activity: Make a family safety checklist with an adult.

New Words: Prevention, immunisation, vaccination, infection, healthcare.

My Healthy Living Promise: I will follow safe health practices and ask trusted adults for help.

CHAPTER SIX

MANAGING STRESS, FEELINGS AND EMOTIONS

Introduction

Children experience different emotions every day.

Sometimes they feel happy and excited. At other times, they may feel worried, disappointed, frightened or angry.

These feelings are normal. What matters is learning healthy ways to understand and manage them.

6.1 Understanding Emotions

Emotions are feelings that arise in response to situations and experiences.

Common emotions include:

- Happiness.
- Sadness.
- Anger.
- Fear.
- Excitement.
- Disappointment.
- Love.

Children should understand that it is acceptable to express their feelings respectfully.

6.2 What Causes Stress?

Children may experience stress because of:

- Difficult schoolwork.
- Examinations.
- Family disagreements.
- Bullying.
- Friendship problems.
- Too many activities.
- Changes in their lives.

6.3 Healthy Ways to Manage Stress

Children can manage ordinary stress by:

1. Talking to trusted adults.
2. Taking slow, comfortable breaths.
3. Playing and engaging in enjoyable activities.
4. Getting sufficient sleep.
5. Organising schoolwork.
6. Spending time with family and friends.
7. Asking for help when tasks become difficult.

6.4 The Importance of Friendship

Good friendships provide companionship, encouragement and support.

Children should choose friends who respect others, behave responsibly and encourage positive activities.

They should avoid bullying, insults and harmful peer pressure.

6.5 When to Ask for Help

If a child feels sad, frightened, worried or overwhelmed for a long time, the child should speak to a parent, teacher, caregiver or qualified health professional.

Children should never be made to feel ashamed of asking for help.

6.6 Learning Self-Control

Self-control means learning to manage our reactions.

For example, instead of fighting when angry, a child can step away, calm down and explain what happened.

Think About It

1. What makes you happy?
2. What can you do when you become angry?
3. Who can you talk to when you feel worried?

Remember: Strong children are not those who never experience difficulties; they are those who learn to seek help and respond positively.

Activity: Draw different facial expressions showing happiness, sadness, anger and surprise.

New Words: Stress, emotions, confidence, friendship, self-control.

My Healthy Living Promise: I will respect my feelings and those of others, and seek help when necessary.

CHAPTER SEVEN

THE IMPORTANCE OF ADEQUATE SLEEP AND REST

Introduction

After a busy day of learning, playing and other activities, our bodies need rest.

Sleep is essential for children's growth, development, memory and emotional well-being.

7.1 Why Do We Need Sleep?

Adequate sleep helps:

- The body grow and repair itself.
- The brain organise and remember information.
- Children concentrate in school.
- The immune system function properly.
- Children manage their emotions.
- The body regain energy.

7.2 How Much Sleep Do Children Need?

Recommended sleep durations generally vary by age:

- Ages 3–5 years: 10–13 hours daily, including naps.
- Ages 6–12 years: 9–12 hours daily.
- Ages 13–18 years: 8–10 hours daily.

Individual needs may vary.

7.3 Developing Good Sleeping Habits

Children should:

- Go to bed at a regular time.
- Wake up at a consistent time.
- Sleep in a comfortable and safe environment.
- Avoid exciting screen activities immediately before bedtime.
- Avoid consuming caffeinated drinks.
- Develop a calming bedtime routine.

7.4 The Importance of Rest

Rest does not always mean sleeping.

Children also need quiet periods during the day to relax, read, think and recover from strenuous activities.

7.5 What Happens When We Do Not Sleep Enough?

Insufficient sleep may cause:

- Tiredness.
- Difficulty concentrating.
- Irritability.
- Poor school performance.
- Reduced energy.
- Difficulty managing emotions.

Think About It

1. Why is sleep important?
2. What time do you normally go to bed?
3. What can you do to improve your sleeping habits?

Remember: Sleep is not wasted time. It is an investment in healthy growth.

Activity: Prepare a bedtime timetable with your family.

New Words: Sleep, rest, relaxation, concentration, routine.

My Healthy Living Promise: I will develop regular sleeping and resting habits.

CHAPTER EIGHT

BALANCING SCHOOL, WORK, PLAY AND FAMILY LIFE

Introduction

Children have different responsibilities.

They attend school, complete assignments, help with appropriate household activities, play with friends and spend time with family.

A healthy life requires a reasonable balance between these activities.

8.1 The Meaning of Balance

Balance means giving appropriate attention to the important activities in our lives without allowing one activity to take over everything.

A child who spends all day studying without enough rest may become exhausted.

Similarly, a child who spends all day playing may neglect schoolwork and other responsibilities.

8.2 Managing Time

Time management means organising activities so that important tasks are completed appropriately.

Children can:

- Follow a daily timetable.
- Complete homework at an appropriate time.
- Set aside time for play.
- Help with household responsibilities.
- Eat meals regularly.
- Get sufficient sleep.

8.3 The Importance of Family Life

Family provides love, care, guidance and support.

Children should spend time talking with family members, sharing experiences and participating in suitable household activities.

8.4 Learning Through Play

Play is important for physical, intellectual and social development.

Through play, children learn:

- Cooperation.
- Creativity.
- Problem-solving.
- Patience.
- Leadership.
- Respect for others.

8.5 Avoiding Too Many Activities

Children should not be overloaded with schoolwork, lessons and other commitments.

Parents and teachers should allow time for rest, recreation and family relationships.

8.6 Digital Balance

Digital devices can support learning and communication.

However, children should use them responsibly, observe age-appropriate screen limits and avoid unsafe online interactions.

Think About It

1. Why is time management important?
2. How can you balance schoolwork and play?
3. Why should children spend time with family?

Remember: A healthy life requires time for learning, responsibility, recreation and rest.

Activity: Prepare a simple daily timetable showing school, meals, homework, play, family time and sleep.

New Words: Balance, responsibility, timetable, recreation, discipline.

My Healthy Living Promise: I will use my time wisely and give attention to my important responsibilities.

CHAPTER NINE

HEALTHY DAILY HABITS AND RESPONSIBLE CHOICES

Introduction

Healthy living is built through the small things we do every day.

A single healthy action may seem unimportant, but repeated good habits can contribute significantly to our well-being.

9.1 Starting the Day Well

A healthy morning routine may include:

- Waking up at an appropriate time.
- Saying a prayer or taking a quiet moment, according to family beliefs.
- Bathing and brushing teeth.
- Eating breakfast.
- Dressing appropriately.
- Preparing for school.

9.2 Drinking Enough Safe Water

Water is essential for the body.

Children should drink water regularly throughout the day, especially during hot weather and physical activity.

9.3 Choosing Healthy Activities

Children should choose activities that support their development, including reading, sports, creative hobbies and helping others.

9.4 Avoiding Harmful Habits

Children should avoid:

- Smoking and tobacco products.
- Alcohol.
- Unprescribed medicines.
- Harmful drugs.
- Excessive consumption of sugary drinks.
- Dangerous games and activities.

9.5 Making Responsible Choices

Responsible choices involve thinking about the consequences of our actions.

For example, choosing clean drinking water instead of an unsafe drink is a responsible health decision.

9.6 Developing Consistency

Healthy habits become easier when practised regularly.

Children should not become discouraged when they make mistakes. They should learn, improve and continue making positive choices.

Think About It

1. What healthy habit do you practise every morning?
2. Why should children avoid harmful substances?
3. How can you encourage your friends to make healthy choices?

Remember: Healthy habits today contribute to a healthier tomorrow.

Activity: Create a seven-day healthy habits chart and tick each habit you practise.

New Words: Habit, responsibility, discipline, consistency, decision.

My Healthy Living Promise: I will practise healthy habits and make responsible choices.

CHAPTER TEN

PERSONAL WELL-BEING: BUILDING A HEALTHY FUTURE

Introduction

Personal well-being means taking care of ourselves in ways that support a healthy, meaningful and satisfying life.

It involves our physical health, mental development, emotional stability, social relationships and personal responsibilities.

10.1 Taking Responsibility for Our Health

Children should learn that they have an important role in maintaining their health.

This includes eating nutritious food, exercising, keeping clean, sleeping well and seeking help when unwell.

10.2 Building Positive Relationships

Healthy relationships are based on:

- Respect.
- Kindness.
- Honesty.
- Cooperation.
- Understanding.
- Forgiveness.

Children should learn to respect differences and treat others fairly.

10.3 Protecting Personal Safety

Children should:

- Avoid dangerous places and situations.
- Never accept unsafe instructions from strangers.
- Tell trusted adults about bullying or abuse.
- Protect their privacy.
- Ask for help when they feel unsafe.

10.4 Caring for the Environment

A healthy environment supports healthy living.

Children can contribute by:

- Keeping their surroundings clean.
- Planting trees and flowers.
- Conserving water.
- Avoiding littering.
- Disposing of waste properly.
- Supporting community cleanliness.

10.5 Becoming Health Ambassadors

A health ambassador is someone who encourages positive health practices.

Children can become health ambassadors by sharing what they learn with their friends, siblings and family members.

They should communicate responsibly and leave medical decisions to qualified professionals.

10.6 My Healthy Future

A healthy future begins with positive choices today.

Every child can contribute to a healthier family, school and community by practising good habits and encouraging others to do the same.

Think About It

1. What does personal well-being mean?
2. How can you contribute to a healthy environment?
3. What healthy habit would you like to teach your friends?

Remember: Take care of yourself, respect others and help build a healthier community.

Activity: Draw a picture of your dream healthy home, school and community.

New Words: Well-being, safety, environment, community, health ambassador.

My Healthy Living Promise: I will protect my health, respect others and contribute to a healthier society.

CONCLUSION

A LIFETIME OF HEALTHY LIVING

Healthy living is a lifelong responsibility that begins in childhood.

The ten chapters of this book have introduced important principles of personal well-being, including balanced nutrition, physical activity, personal hygiene, preventive healthcare, emotional health, adequate sleep, responsible time management and healthy daily habits.

Children should understand that good health is not achieved through one action or one day of effort. It develops through regular practice, responsible decisions and support from parents, teachers, healthcare professionals and communities.

The lessons in this book should therefore become part of everyday family and school life.

Every child deserves the opportunity to grow, learn, play and develop in a healthy environment.

A healthy child today is better prepared to become a healthy, productive and responsible adult tomorrow.

3. Glossary

Balanced diet: Eating different nutritious foods in appropriate proportions.

Exercise: Physical activity that helps keep the body fit.

Emotional health: The ability to understand and manage feelings.

Fitness: The condition of being physically active and capable.

Germs: Microscopic organisms, some of which can cause disease.

Healthcare: Services and activities that protect and improve health.

Hygiene: Practices that maintain personal cleanliness and health.

Immunisation: Protection against certain diseases through vaccination.

Nutrition: The process through which the body obtains and uses nutrients.

Preventive healthcare: Actions taken to prevent illness and identify health problems early.

Stress: A response to challenging or demanding situations.

Well-being: A person's overall physical, mental, emotional and social welfare.
