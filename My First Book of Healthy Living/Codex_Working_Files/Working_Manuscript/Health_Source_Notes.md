# Health-source notes for the working manuscript

This note records primary/official sources used to check quantitative and medical guidance in Version 1. It is a research record for editors and reviewers, not a substitute for a qualified Nigerian health professional's sign-off.

| Source | Manuscript use | Editorial limitation |
|---|---|---|
| WHO, *Guidelines on Physical Activity and Sedentary Behaviour* (2020), https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240015128 | Chapter 3: WHO's average 60-minutes-per-day physical activity recommendation for ages 5–17 across the week; inclusive adaptations for disability/health conditions. | Keep ages explicit; recommendations are public-health guidance, not a requirement used to shame an individual child. |
| WHO and UNICEF, *Guidelines on Hand Hygiene in Community Settings* (2025), https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240116559 | Chapter 4: plain soap and water, key times and thorough rubbing of both hands. | The current guideline does not need an unsupported fixed-time claim; the draft does not say “exactly 20 seconds.” Access to soap/water is a shared responsibility. |
| WHO, “Oral Health” (17 March 2025), https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/oral-health | Chapter 4: brushing twice a day with fluoride toothpaste and seeking dental guidance. | Amount and fluoride concentration depend on age and local circumstances; direct caregivers to age-appropriate dental advice rather than prescribing a toothpaste amount. |
| WHO, “Malaria” Q&A (4 December 2025), https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/malaria | Chapter 5: insecticide-treated nets as an effective prevention measure; fever needs assessment/testing by a health provider. | Do not imply every fever is malaria or direct children to self-treat. Local Nigerian malaria advice can change and should be confirmed at proof. |
| Paruthi et al., “Recommended Amount of Sleep for Pediatric Populations” (2016), https://doi.org/10.5664/jcsm.5866 | Chapter 7: 3–5 years, 10–13 h including naps; 6–12 years, 9–12 h; 13–18 years, 8–10 h. | These are recommended regular durations; the draft notes individual variation and seeks adult/clinical help for persistent problems. |
| WHO, “Healthy Diet” (26 January 2026), https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet | Chapter 2: variety, adequacy, balance, moderation, diversity and safe food; avoids prescribing one plate for every child. | Food access, culture, allergies and medical needs vary. A child should not be blamed or put on a restrictive diet from a book. |
| WHO, *mhGAP Intervention Guide, Version 2.0* (2016), https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241549790 | Chapter 6: support and referral boundaries for persistent emotional difficulty. | The guide is for health workers, not children. The manuscript uses it only to support the principle that children need trusted adults and qualified care—not to teach self-diagnosis. |
| National Primary Health Care Development Agency (Nigeria), “Frequently Asked Questions: Immunization,” https://nphcda.gov.ng/faqs/ | Chapter 5: refer caregivers to Nigeria's official immunisation guidance. | Always check the current national schedule; schedules and campaigns may change. |

## Fact-check questions for final proof

- Ask a qualified Nigerian child-health or public-health reviewer to review nutrition, medicine, immunisation, malaria, oral-health, safety and emotional-support passages.
- Confirm current local immunisation and malaria guidance immediately before final publication.
- Check that illustrations do not imply self-medication, unsafe food handling, unsupervised water/road activity or guaranteed health outcomes.
- Keep references and edition metadata current if a later edition is revised.
