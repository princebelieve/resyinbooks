# Children's-book art direction and illustration briefs

## Visual system

- **Reader:** primarily ages 7–12; younger children may read with an adult.
- **Look:** warm, lively picture-book illustration with clear shapes, expressive faces, gentle humor and believable everyday settings. Use the supplied Healthy Living cover and chapter overview as subject/style references, not as interior pages to reproduce.
- **People:** Nigerian children with varied deep and medium-brown skin tones, natural hair styles, varied body types, and a mix of girls and boys. Show children with different abilities naturally, including mobility aids where appropriate. Avoid making disability, poverty, illness or body size a visual joke or moral lesson.
- **Continuity:** use a small recurring cast—Ada, Tobi, Amina, Emeka, Sade, Bisi, Kunle, Mariam and Zainab—matching each chapter's text. Keep clothing/colors/appearance consistent whenever the same child returns.
- **Health framing:** show safe, supportive adult involvement where the story asks for it. No medical procedures, medicines, brands, unsafe handling, fear-based imagery, shaming, or frightening disease scenes.
- **Layout:** one full-page or large half-page scene per chapter opener, plus small spot illustrations for activities and glossary/pledge pages. Leave clear quiet space near edges for page layout. No text, letters, labels, logos, watermarks or embedded captions inside generated art; set book text in the page layout.
- **Accessibility:** illustrations support the lesson but never carry essential information alone. Provide concise, useful alt text for digital editions.

## Chapter illustration briefs

For each prompt, use the matching native-size `Illustration_References/Chapter_XX_Storyboard_Crop.png` as a visual reference. Recreate its specific lesson in one clean, full-resolution scene; do not copy the tiny raster text or repeat the complete mini-page. Keep character design, clothing, setting and lighting consistent across the ten scenes.

1. **Four parts of well-being:** Four children in a welcoming everyday home/school setting, as in the source card. Their activity, learning, expressions and friendship convey physical, mental, emotional and social well-being. No embedded words or floating labels.
2. **Food and growth:** Tobi and Amina help an adult arrange familiar foods such as rice, beans, leafy vegetables, oranges, yam and safe drinking water for a shared meal. Show variety without a rigid “perfect plate” or food hierarchy.
3. **Movement and play:** Emeka and Sade play a cooperative ball game in a safe schoolyard; Sade has a temporary ankle injury and joins from a seated position as scorekeeper/strategist, included by friends. Include a wheelchair-using classmate taking part in the passing game. No one is pressured to run.
4. **Clean habits:** a child washes hands with soap at a school handwashing station while a classmate waits; show clean water, soap and correct hand coverage without diagrammatic text.
5. **Health and safety:** A calm three-part visual: caregiver and nurse discuss preventive care, a child drinks safe water, and an adult makes a mosquito net ready. Show no close-up of a needle. Keep medicines and chemicals out of children’s reach; no child handles unknown substances.
6. **Feelings and support:** Bisi talks with a trusted older cousin and teacher in a calm, private but safe school setting; listening body language and reassurance, no bullying scene shown graphically.
7. **Sleep and rest:** Kunle follows a calm bedtime routine—books packed, device put away, a book by the bed—with a caregiver nearby. Warm evening light; safe, comfortable room without assuming a particular household size or wealth.
8. **Balance:** Mariam's illustrated weekly plan with school, homework, play, family meal, rest and an open “flex time” space. Any written schedule will be added in layout, not in the image.
9. **Daily habits:** Zainab follows a morning-and-evening routine with caregiver support: breakfast, water, movement, clean clothes/teeth, fruit and vegetables, and a kind responsible choice. A small weekly tracker may frame the scene; no grades or shame.
10. **Healthy future/community:** children and adults care for a school garden after an adult has made the space safe; children water plants and invite a classmate into the activity. Show shared responsibility and adult supervision.
