Chapter Twelve

Registers and Idiomatic Expressions

Learning Objectives

At the end of this chapter, students should be able to:

- Define register and idiomatic expression.
- Identify vocabulary associated with different professions and fields.
- Use common English idioms correctly.
- Distinguish between literal and idiomatic meanings.
- Answer WAEC and NECO questions on registers and idiomatic expressions with confidence.

---

12.1 Meaning of Register

A register is the specialised vocabulary or set of words associated with a particular profession, occupation, discipline or activity.

Understanding registers enables candidates to identify the correct words used in different fields.

---

12.2 Legal Register

Common legal terms include:

Word| Meaning
Judge| Presides over a court
Lawyer| Legal practitioner
Witness| Gives evidence
Defendant| Person accused in court
Plaintiff| Person bringing a civil action
Verdict| Court's decision
Appeal| Request for review of a judgment
Bail| Temporary release from custody
Evidence| Proof presented in court
Sentence| Punishment imposed by a court

12.11 Idiomatic Expressions

An idiomatic expression is a group of words whose meaning is different from the literal meanings of the individual words.

Common Idioms

Idiom| Meaning
Break the ice| Start a conversation
Hit the nail on the head| Be exactly right
Spill the beans| Reveal a secret
Once in a blue moon| Very rarely
Burn the midnight oil| Study or work late
Cost an arm and a leg| Very expensive
At the eleventh hour| At the last moment
Keep an eye on| Watch carefully
Bite the bullet| Face a difficult situation courageously
Pull someone's leg| Tease someone jokingly
Barking up the wrong tree| Pursuing the wrong idea
A blessing in disguise| Something good that first seemed bad
Add fuel to the fire| Make a bad situation worse
Under the weather| Feeling unwell
Let the cat out of the bag| Reveal a secret unintentionally

---

12.12 Importance of Registers and Idioms

Knowledge of registers and idioms helps students to:

- Improve vocabulary.
- Communicate appropriately in different situations.
- Understand comprehension passages.
- Write better essays and letters.
- Perform well in WAEC and NECO objective questions.

---

12.13 Common Errors

Avoid:

- Interpreting idioms literally.
- Using legal vocabulary in medical contexts.
- Confusing banking terms with commercial terms.
- Mixing registers from different professions.

---

Examination Tips

- Learn specialised vocabulary from different fields.
- Study at least five new idioms every week.
- Read newspapers, magazines and textbooks to observe registers in context.
- Practise using idiomatic expressions in your own sentences.

---

Chapter Summary

Registers are specialised vocabularies associated with particular professions, while idiomatic expressions are fixed expressions with meanings different from the literal meanings of their words. Mastering both areas enhances communication skills and improves performance in WAEC and NECO English Language examinations.

WAEC/NECO Revision Questions

Objective Questions

Choose the correct answer.

1. The word verdict belongs to the:
   a. Medical register
   b. Legal register
   c. Banking register
   d. Agricultural register

2. Which of the following belongs to the banking register?
   a. Fertiliser
   b. Deposit
   c. Vaccine
   d. Stadium

3. "Burn the midnight oil" means:
   a. Set a fire at night
   b. Work or study late into the night
   c. Waste electricity
   d. Sleep late

4. A pharmacy belongs to which register?
   a. Sports
   b. Education
   c. Medical
   d. Commerce

5. "At the eleventh hour" means:
   a. Early in the morning
   b. At the last possible moment
   c. Exactly at eleven o'clock
   d. During a meeting

---

Theory Questions

2. Explain the importance of registers in communication.

WAEC/NECO Examination Tips

- Read widely to become familiar with specialised vocabulary from different professions.
- Learn idiomatic expressions as complete units rather than translating them word for word.
- Pay close attention to context when answering Lexis and Structure questions.
- Revise registers and idioms regularly alongside grammar and vocabulary.
- Practise with past WAEC and NECO questions to reinforce your understanding.
