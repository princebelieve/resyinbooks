Chapter Two

Grammar: Parts of Speech

Learning Objectives

At the end of this chapter, students should be able to:

- Define grammar and explain its importance.
- Identify the eight major parts of speech.
- Use each part of speech correctly in speech and writing.
- Recognise common grammatical errors.
- Answer WAEC and NECO grammar questions with confidence.

2.1 Meaning of Grammar

Grammar is the system of rules that governs the correct use of a language. It explains how words are formed and arranged into meaningful sentences. Good grammar promotes effective communication and is essential for success in WAEC and NECO English Language examinations.

2.2 The Parts of Speech

The parts of speech are the categories into which words are classified according to their functions in a sentence.

The major parts of speech are:

1. Noun
2. Pronoun
3. Verb
4. Adjective
5. Adverb
6. Preposition
7. Conjunction
8. Interjection

---

2.3 Nouns

A noun is the name of a person, place, animal, thing or idea.

Types of Nouns

(a) Proper Nouns

These are specific names and begin with capital letters.

Examples:

- Nigeria
- Lagos
- Benin City
- Amina
- River Niger

(b) Common Nouns

These refer to general names.

Examples:

- teacher
- boy
- market
- school
- river

(c) Concrete Nouns

Names of things that can be seen or touched.

Examples:

- table
- book
- dog
- chair

(d) Abstract Nouns

Names of qualities, ideas or feelings.

Examples:

- honesty
- courage
- wisdom
- happiness

(e) Collective Nouns

Names of groups.

Examples:

- team
- committee
- family
- jury
- crowd

---

2.4 Pronouns

A pronoun replaces a noun to avoid unnecessary repetition.

Examples:

- I
- you
- he
- she
- it
- we
- they

Types of Pronouns

- Personal Pronouns
- Possessive Pronouns
- Reflexive Pronouns
- Demonstrative Pronouns
- Relative Pronouns
- Interrogative Pronouns
- Indefinite Pronouns

Example:

John is a brilliant student. He studies every day.

---

2.5 Verbs

A verb expresses an action or a state of being.

Examples:

- run
- write
- eat
- sing
- is
- was
- become

Types of Verbs

- Action Verbs
- Linking Verbs
- Auxiliary (Helping) Verbs
- Modal Verbs

Example:

The students are writing the examination.

---

2.6 Adjectives

An adjective describes or qualifies a noun or pronoun.

Examples:

- beautiful girl
- intelligent student
- tall building
- blue shirt
- five books

Degrees of Comparison

Positive: tall

Comparative: taller

Superlative: tallest

Example:

James is taller than David.

John is the tallest student in the class.

---

2.7 Adverbs

An adverb modifies a verb, an adjective or another adverb.

Examples:

- quickly
- slowly
- carefully
- yesterday
- very
- quite

Example:

The teacher spoke clearly.

---

2.8 Prepositions

A preposition shows the relationship between a noun (or pronoun) and another word.

Common prepositions include:

- in
- on
- under
- above
- beside
- between
- among
- during
- across
- into

Examples:

The book is on the table.

She travelled to Abuja.

---

2.9 Conjunctions

A conjunction joins words, phrases or clauses.

Common conjunctions include:

- and
- but
- or
- because
- although
- since
- while
- if

Examples:

Peter and Paul are friends.

She studied hard because she wanted to pass.

---

2.10 Interjections

An interjection expresses sudden emotion.

Examples:

- Oh!
- Wow!
- Alas!
- Hurrah!
- Ouch!

Example:

Hurrah! We have won the competition.

---

2.11 Common WAEC/NECO Errors

Incorrect: She don't like rice.

Correct: She doesn't like rice.

Incorrect: The committee have agreed.

Correct: The committee has agreed.

Incorrect: He is more taller than James.

Correct: He is taller than James.

Incorrect: I am going in school.

Correct: I am going to school.

Incorrect: He did the work good.

Correct: He did the work well.

---

Examination Tips

- Identify the function of the underlined word before choosing your answer.
- Read the whole sentence carefully.
- Watch out for tense and subject-verb agreement.
- Eliminate obviously incorrect options first.
- Practise grammar exercises daily.

---

Chapter Summary

Grammar is the foundation of effective communication. A sound knowledge of the parts of speech enables students to construct correct sentences and perform well in the Lexis and Structure section of WAEC and NECO examinations.

WAEC/NECO Revision Questions

Objective Questions

1. Which part of speech names a person, place or thing?
   a. Verb
   b. Noun
   c. Adverb
   d. Preposition

2. Which of the following is a pronoun?
   a. Honest
   b. Quickly
   c. They
   d. School

3. A word that describes a noun is called a:
   a. Verb
   b. Adjective
   c. Conjunction
   d. Interjection

4. Which of the following is a preposition?
   a. Under
   b. Quickly
   c. Beautiful
   d. Laugh

5. Which sentence is correct?
   a. She don't know him.
   b. They was happy.
   c. He writes neatly.
   d. I am going in school.

Theory Questions

1. Define grammar and explain its importance.
2. Explain the eight parts of speech with examples.
3. Distinguish between common nouns and proper nouns.
4. Discuss the functions of verbs, adjectives and adverbs.
5. Correct the grammatical errors in the following sentences:
   - The boy don't like football.
   - She sings beautiful.
   - They was waiting for us.
   - He is the most tallest player.
   - We discussed about the matter.

Practical Exercise

Identify the part of speech of each underlined word in the following sentences:

1. Mary won the scholarship.
2. The children played happily.
3. The old man walked into the room.
4. Wow! That was amazing.
5. The diligent student passed the examination because he studied consistently.
