Chapter Fifteen

Complete Mock Examinations, Marking Guide and Final Revision Guide

Learning Objectives

At the end of this chapter, students should be able to:

- Assess their level of preparedness for WAEC and NECO English Language examinations.
- Identify areas requiring further revision.
- Improve speed, accuracy and confidence.
- Develop an effective examination strategy for achieving an excellent grade.

---

15.1 Introduction

Revision is most effective when students practise under conditions similar to the actual examination. The two mock examinations in this chapter are original practice papers designed to test grammar, vocabulary, comprehension, summary writing, essay writing and Oral English.

Candidates are advised to complete each paper within the prescribed examination time without consulting textbooks or notes.

Choose the correct answer.

B. seat

C. set

D. sat

B. were

C. is

---

Section B: Comprehension

Read the passage provided by your teacher or prepare one of similar length.

Answer questions on:

- Main idea.
- Supporting details.
- Vocabulary in context.
- Inference.
- Writer's attitude.

---

Section C: Summary

Summarise a passage of about 350–450 words by identifying the principal ideas and expressing them clearly and concisely in your own words where required.

---

Section D: Essay Writing

Write one essay of about 450 words on any one of the following topics.

1. Education as a Tool for National Development.
2. My Most Memorable Day.
3. The Effects of Examination Malpractice.
4. Write a letter to your Local Government Chairman requesting improvements in public healthcare.

General Marking Guide

Although the exact marking scheme varies from year to year, examiners generally assess candidates on:

Essay Writing

- Relevance to the topic.
- Organisation of ideas.
- Content.
- Expression.
- Grammar.
- Vocabulary.
- Mechanical accuracy (punctuation and spelling).

Letter Writing

- Correct format.
- Appropriate tone.
- Logical development.
- Language and expression.
- Accuracy.

Comprehension

- Correct interpretation.
- Accuracy of answers.
- Appropriate expression.
- Vocabulary.

Summary Writing

- Identification of main points.
- Brevity.
- Clarity.
- Use of own words where required.
- Grammatical accuracy.

Objective Questions

- One mark is awarded for each correct answer.
- There is no award for partially correct answers.

---

Common Reasons Candidates Lose Marks

- Poor handwriting.
- Weak grammar.
- Poor paragraphing.
- Failure to answer the exact question.
- Poor time management.
- Inadequate vocabulary.
- Incorrect letter format.
- Careless spelling mistakes.
- Failure to revise completed work.

---

A 30-Day Revision Plan

Week One

- Revise Grammar.
- Study Concord.
- Practise Lexis and Structure.

Week Two

- Revise Comprehension.
- Practise Summary Writing.
- Learn new vocabulary and idioms.

Week Three

- Write at least four essays.
- Practise formal, informal and semi-formal letters.
- Revise Oral English.

Week Four

- Attempt two complete mock examinations.
- Review mistakes.
- Revise difficult topics.
- Read extensively each day.

---

Examination Day Checklist

Before leaving for the examination:

- Bring your examination slip.
- Bring approved writing materials.
- Arrive at the venue early.
- Read the instructions carefully.
- Allocate your time wisely.
- Write clearly and neatly.
- Attempt every compulsory question.
- Review your work before submitting your script.

---

Final Advice to Candidates

Success in English Language is not achieved through memorisation alone. It requires:

- Regular reading.
- Daily writing practice.
- Consistent grammar revision.
- Vocabulary development.
- Careful listening and speaking.
- Continuous practice with examination-style questions.

Confidence grows from preparation. Students who study consistently, practise regularly and manage their examination time effectively greatly improve their chances of earning excellent grades.
