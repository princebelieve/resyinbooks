Chapter Nine

Letter Writing

Learning Objectives

At the end of this chapter, students should be able to:

- Define letter writing.
- Distinguish between formal, informal and semi-formal letters.
- Apply the correct format for each type of letter.
- Write clear, coherent and grammatically correct letters.
- Answer WAEC and NECO letter-writing questions confidently.

---

9.1 Meaning of Letter Writing

A letter is a written means of communication between one person and another, or between an individual and an organisation.

Letter writing is an important aspect of the WAEC and NECO English Language examination because it tests candidates' ability to communicate effectively in real-life situations.

---

9.2 Types of Letters

There are three major types of letters:

1. Informal Letter
2. Formal Letter
3. Semi-formal Letter

---

9.3 Informal Letter

An informal letter is written to people with whom the writer has a close relationship, such as:

- Parents
- Brothers and sisters
- Friends
- Cousins
- Close relatives

Features of an Informal Letter

- Writer's address
- Date
- Salutation (e.g. Dear John,)
- Friendly introduction
- Body of the letter
- Conclusion
- Complimentary close (e.g. Yours sincerely, or Your loving son,)
- Writer's name

Sample Question

Write a letter to your friend explaining how you spent your last long vacation.

---

9.4 Formal Letter

A formal letter is written to:

- Government officials
- School principals
- Company managers
- Organisations
- Institutions
- Newspaper editors

Features of a Formal Letter

- Writer's address
- Date
- Recipient's designation and address
- Salutation (Dear Sir, or Dear Madam,)
- Title or subject of the letter
- Body of the letter
- Complimentary close (Yours faithfully,)
- Signature
- Full name

Sample Question

Write a letter to your school principal requesting permission to organise a debate competition.

---

9.5 Semi-formal Letter

A semi-formal letter is written to people who are known to the writer but with whom a respectful relationship exists.

Examples include:

- Teachers
- Religious leaders
- Family doctors
- Employers
- Community leaders

The language is polite but less rigid than that of a formal letter.

---

9.6 Qualities of a Good Letter

A good letter should:

- Follow the correct format.
- Address the topic fully.
- Be clear and concise.
- Use appropriate language.
- Observe correct grammar and punctuation.
- Have properly organised paragraphs.
- Maintain the appropriate tone.

---

9.7 Common Errors in Letter Writing

Avoid the following mistakes:

- Wrong address format.
- Omitting the date.
- Wrong salutation.
- Mixing formal and informal language.
- Poor paragraphing.
- Grammatical errors.
- Incorrect complimentary close.
- Writing off the topic.

---

9.8 Sample Informal Letter

Question

Write a letter to your elder brother describing your new school.

Outline

Introduction

- Greeting.
- Expression of goodwill.

Body

- Description of the school.
- Teachers.
- Facilities.
- Academic activities.
- Extracurricular activities.
- Personal experiences.

Conclusion

- Appreciation.
- Best wishes.
- Closing remarks.

---

9.9 Sample Formal Letter

Question

Write a letter to the Chairman of your Local Government requesting the repair of roads in your community.

Outline

Introduction

- Introduce yourself.
- State the purpose of writing.

Body

- Describe the poor condition of the roads.
- Explain the effects on residents.
- Request urgent government intervention.

Conclusion

- Express appreciation.
- Hope for prompt action.

---

9.10 Examination Tips

- Read the question carefully.
- Identify the type of letter required.
- Follow the correct format.
- Use language appropriate to the audience.
- Develop each paragraph logically.
- Revise your work before submission.

---

Chapter Summary

Letter writing is an essential communication skill tested in WAEC and NECO. Candidates should master the formats of informal, formal and semi-formal letters, use appropriate language and ensure that every letter is well organised, grammatically correct and relevant to the question.

---

WAEC/NECO Revision Questions

Objective Questions

Choose the correct answer.

1. A letter written to a close friend is:
   a. Formal
   b. Informal
   c. Official
   d. Commercial

2. The correct complimentary close for a formal letter is:
   a. Yours lovingly
   b. Yours sincerely
   c. Yours faithfully
   d. Best regards

3. A formal letter should be addressed to:
   a. A close friend
   b. A younger brother
   c. A school principal
   d. A cousin

4. Which of the following should appear in a formal letter?
   a. Recipient's address
   b. Nickname
   c. Slang expressions
   d. Personal jokes

5. Semi-formal letters are usually written to:
   a. Complete strangers only
   b. Close family members only
   c. People known to the writer in an official or respectful relationship
   d. Children only

---

Theory Questions

1. Define letter writing.
2. Distinguish between formal, informal and semi-formal letters.
3. State the features of a formal letter.
4. Explain five qualities of a good letter.
5. Identify five common errors made in letter writing.

---

Practical Exercise

Answer one of the following questions.

1. Write a letter to your friend explaining how you prepared for your WAEC or NECO examination.

2. Write a letter to your principal suggesting three ways of improving discipline in your school.

3. Write a letter to the Chairman of your Local Government requesting improved waste disposal and environmental sanitation in your community.

---

WAEC/NECO Examination Tips

- Determine the type of letter before you begin writing.
- Observe the correct layout and format.
- Maintain an appropriate tone throughout the letter.
- Support your ideas with relevant details.
- Check your spelling, grammar and punctuation before submitting your script.
