Chapter Three

Tenses and Sentence Structure

Learning Objectives

At the end of this chapter, students should be able to:

- Explain the concept of tense.
- Identify and correctly use the twelve English tenses.
- Construct grammatically correct sentences.
- Distinguish between active and passive voice.
- Change direct speech to indirect speech and vice versa.
- Avoid common sentence errors tested in WAEC and NECO examinations.

---

3.1 Meaning of Tense

A tense is the form of a verb that shows the time an action takes place. The action may occur in the present, past, or future.

Correct use of tense is essential for speaking and writing accurate English.

---

3.2 The Twelve English Tenses

The English language has twelve major tenses.

A. Present Tenses

1. Simple Present Tense

Structure: Subject + Base Verb (+ s/es)

Examples

- She teaches English.
- They play football every Saturday.
- The sun rises in the east.

Uses

- Habitual actions.
- General truths.
- Permanent situations.

---

2. Present Continuous Tense

Structure: Subject + is/am/are + Verb + ing

Examples:

- I am reading.
- They are studying.
- She is cooking dinner.

Uses:

- Actions happening now.
- Temporary activities.

---

3. Present Perfect Tense

Structure: Subject + has/have + Past Participle

Examples:

- I have completed my assignment.
- She has travelled to Abuja.
- They have finished the work.

Uses:

- Actions completed recently.
- Actions whose effects remain.

---

4. Present Perfect Continuous

Structure: Subject + has/have been + Verb + ing

Examples:

- He has been reading for two hours.
- We have been waiting since morning.

---

B. Past Tenses

5. Simple Past Tense

Examples:

- She visited Benin yesterday.
- They arrived early.
- I wrote the examination last week.

---

6. Past Continuous Tense

Structure:

Subject + was/were + Verb + ing

Examples:

- They were playing football.
- She was sleeping when I arrived.

---

7. Past Perfect Tense

Structure:

Subject + had + Past Participle

Examples:

- The train had left before we arrived.
- She had completed the work.

---

8. Past Perfect Continuous

Structure:

Subject + had been + Verb + ing

Examples:

- He had been teaching for ten years.
- They had been waiting for several hours.

---

C. Future Tenses

9. Simple Future Tense

Structure:

Subject + shall/will + Base Verb

Examples:

- I will visit Lagos tomorrow.
- She will succeed.

---

10. Future Continuous

Structure:

Subject + will be + Verb + ing

Examples:

- We will be travelling tomorrow.
- She will be reading at this time.

---

11. Future Perfect

Structure:

Subject + will have + Past Participle

Examples:

- They will have completed the project.
- I will have finished the examination.

---

12. Future Perfect Continuous

Structure:

Subject + will have been + Verb + ing

Examples:

- She will have been teaching for twenty years next month.
- We will have been living here for ten years.

---

3.3 Sentence Structure

A sentence is a group of words that expresses a complete thought.

Every complete sentence has:

- Subject
- Predicate

Example:

The students passed the examination.

Subject: The students

Predicate: passed the examination.

---

3.4 Types of Sentences

Declarative Sentence

Makes a statement.

Example:

The examination begins at 9 a.m.

---

Interrogative Sentence

Asks a question.

Example:

Have you completed your assignment?

---

Imperative Sentence

Gives a command or instruction.

Example:

Close the door.

---

Exclamatory Sentence

Expresses strong emotion.

Example:

What a wonderful performance!

---

3.5 Active and Passive Voice

Active Voice

The subject performs the action.

Example:

The teacher marked the scripts.

Passive Voice

The subject receives the action.

Example:

The scripts were marked by the teacher.

More Examples

Active: The police arrested the suspect.

Passive: The suspect was arrested by the police.

Active: She wrote the letter.

Passive: The letter was written by her.

---

3.6 Direct and Indirect Speech

Direct Speech

The speaker's exact words are quoted.

Example:

Mary said, "I am happy."

Indirect Speech

The speaker's words are reported without quotation marks.

Example:

Mary said that she was happy.

Rules

- Change pronouns where necessary.
- Change tense appropriately.
- Remove quotation marks.
- Use reporting verbs correctly.

---

3.7 Common Sentence Errors

Incorrect: He don't understand.

Correct: He doesn't understand.

Incorrect: She have gone home.

Correct: She has gone home.

Incorrect: They was late.

Correct: They were late.

Incorrect: I am knowing him.

Correct: I know him.

Incorrect: He did not went.

Correct: He did not go.

---

3.8 Examination Tips

- Identify time expressions before choosing the tense.
- Read every sentence carefully.
- Ensure subject-verb agreement.
- Avoid mixing tenses unnecessarily.
- Practise changing active voice to passive voice regularly.
- Learn the rules governing reported speech.

---

Chapter Summary

Correct use of tense and sentence structure is fundamental to good English. Students who master the twelve tenses, sentence patterns, active and passive voice, and reported speech are well prepared for the Lexis and Structure section of WAEC and NECO examinations.

WAEC/NECO Revision Questions

Objective Questions

1. Which tense is used for actions happening now?
   a. Simple Past
   b. Present Continuous
   c. Future Perfect
   d. Past Perfect

2. Choose the correct sentence.
   a. She have finished.
   b. They was playing.
   c. He has completed the work.
   d. I am knowing him.

3. Which sentence is in the passive voice?
   a. The boy kicked the ball.
   b. The ball was kicked by the boy.
   c. The boy is kicking the ball.
   d. The boy will kick the ball.

4. Which sentence is in direct speech?
   a. John said that he was tired.
   b. John said, "I am tired."
   c. John was tired.
   d. John had been tired.

5. Every complete sentence must contain:
   a. A paragraph
   b. A subject and a predicate
   c. Two verbs
   d. An adjective

---

Theory Questions

1. Define tense and explain its importance in English grammar.
2. Describe the twelve English tenses with suitable examples.
3. Distinguish between active and passive voice with examples.
4. Explain the differences between direct and indirect speech.
5. Identify and correct the grammatical errors in the following sentences:
   - She have finished her assignment.
   - They was playing football.
   - He did not wrote the examination.
   - I am seeing him every day.
   - The books was arranged neatly.
