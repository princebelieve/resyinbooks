Chapter Ten

Oral English

Learning Objectives

At the end of this chapter, students should be able to:

- Explain the meaning and importance of Oral English.
- Identify English vowel and consonant sounds.
- Recognise diphthongs.
- Apply the principles of stress and intonation.
- Distinguish between minimal pairs.
- Answer WAEC and NECO Oral English questions confidently.

---

10.1 Meaning of Oral English

Oral English is the spoken aspect of the English Language. It deals with the correct pronunciation of words, stress patterns, rhythm and intonation.

In WAEC and NECO examinations, Oral English is usually tested through objective questions that assess pronunciation and sound discrimination.

---

10.2 The English Speech Sounds

English speech sounds are divided into:

- Vowel sounds
- Consonant sounds
- Diphthongs

---

10.3 Vowel Sounds

A vowel sound is produced without any obstruction to the flow of air from the lungs.

English has 20 vowel sounds, made up of:

(A) Twelve Pure Vowels (Monophthongs)

Examples:

Sound| Example Word
/iː/| seat
/ɪ/| sit
/e/| pen
/æ/| cat
/ɑː/| father
/ɒ/| pot (British pronunciation)
/ɔː/| law
/ʊ/| book
/uː/| food
/ʌ/| cup
/ɜː/| bird
/ə/| about (first syllable)

---

(B) Eight Diphthongs

A diphthong is a combination of two vowel sounds pronounced within one syllable.

Examples:

Sound| Example Word
/eɪ/| day
/aɪ/| time
/ɔɪ/| boy
/əʊ/| go
/aʊ/| house
/ɪə/| here
/eə/| care
/ʊə/| tourist (in many traditional British pronunciations)

---

10.5 Minimal Pairs

Minimal pairs are pairs of words that differ by only one sound.

Examples:

Word 1| Word 2
ship| sheep
sit| seat
full| fool
fan| van
pin| bin
cap| cup
light| right
think| sink
bat| bad
coat| goat

Practising minimal pairs helps improve pronunciation and listening skills.

---

10.6 Word Stress

Stress is the extra force given to a particular syllable in a word.

Examples:

- TAble
- WINdow
- TEAcher
- imPORtant
- eduCAtion
- examiNAtion

Incorrect stress may change the natural rhythm of speech and sometimes make communication difficult.

---

10.7 Sentence Stress

In spoken English, important words usually receive more emphasis than function words.

Example:

The STUDENTS PASSED the EXAMINATION.

The words students, passed and examination carry the main stress.

---

10.8 Intonation

Intonation is the rise and fall of the voice during speech.

Falling Intonation

Used in:

- Statements
- Commands
- WH-questions

Example:

Where are you going?

Rising Intonation

Used in:

- Yes/No questions
- Expressions of doubt or surprise

Example:

Are you ready?

---

10.9 Common Pronunciation Errors

Incorrect Pronunciation| Correct Pronunciation
libary| library
goverment| government
Febuary| February
pronounciation| pronunciation
expecially| especially

Students should pronounce words carefully and avoid adding or omitting sounds.

---

10.10 Examination Tips

- Listen carefully before selecting an answer.
- Practise pronunciation every day.
- Learn the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) symbols.
- Read aloud regularly.
- Use a good English dictionary with phonetic transcriptions.
- Pay attention to stress patterns in multisyllabic words.

---

Chapter Summary

Oral English focuses on the correct pronunciation of English sounds, stress, intonation and listening skills. Consistent practice in speaking and listening enables candidates to perform well in the Oral English section of WAEC and NECO examinations.

WAEC/NECO Revision Questions

Objective Questions

Choose the correct answer.

1. Oral English is mainly concerned with:
   a. Essay writing
   b. Pronunciation and speech
   c. Summary writing
   d. Letter writing

2. A diphthong is:
   a. A consonant cluster
   b. Two vowel sounds pronounced in one syllable
   c. Two words with the same meaning
   d. A silent letter

3. Which pair is a minimal pair?
   a. pen – pencil
   b. ship – sheep
   c. table – chair
   d. market – school

4. Falling intonation is commonly used in:
   a. Yes/No questions
   b. Statements
   c. Expressions of surprise only
   d. Greetings only

5. Which word contains the /ʃ/ sound?
   a. chair
   b. shoe
   c. judge
   d. zoo

---

Theory Questions

1. Define Oral English.
2. Distinguish between vowel and consonant sounds.
3. Explain the meaning of:
   - Diphthong
   - Minimal pair
   - Stress
   - Intonation
4. Discuss the importance of correct pronunciation in communication.
5. Explain how students can improve their spoken English.

---

Practical Exercise

1. Pronounce the following pairs of words and identify the differing sounds:
   
   - ship – sheep
   - full – fool
   - fan – van
   - cap – cup
   - think – sink

2. Underline the stressed syllable in each word:
   
   - examination
   - education
   - committee
   - opportunity
   - responsible

3. State whether the following sentences normally take rising or falling intonation:
   
   - Have you finished your homework?
   - Where did you buy the book?
   - Please sit down.
   - Is your brother at home?
   - What is your name?

---

WAEC/NECO Examination Tips

- Read English passages aloud every day to improve pronunciation.
- Learn and practise the IPA symbols commonly tested in WAEC and NECO.
- Listen to proficient speakers of English on radio, television or educational platforms.
- Pay attention to word stress, sentence stress and intonation.
- Revise past WAEC and NECO Oral English questions regularly to become familiar with examination patterns.
