# Chapter One

## Introduction to English Language

### Learning objectives

By the end of this chapter, you should be able to:

- explain why English Language is an important school subject and communication tool;
- identify the broad skills assessed in senior secondary English examinations;
- describe the main areas you need to revise;
- plan regular, balanced preparation; and
- use practical strategies before and during an examination.

### 1.1 English as a language for communication

Language helps people share information, ask questions, explain ideas, tell stories and work together. English is used in many Nigerian classrooms, books, public services and workplaces. It is also a subject in the WAEC and NECO Senior School Certificate Examinations.

Learning English does not require you to abandon Nigerian languages or cultures. People can use more than one language, and each language can be valuable. This handbook focuses on the English skills required for school, examinations and wider communication.

Good communication involves more than memorising grammar rules. It includes listening carefully, speaking clearly, reading with understanding and writing in a way that suits the purpose and audience. Grammar, vocabulary and pronunciation help you use those skills accurately.

### 1.2 Why English Language matters

English can help you to:

1. understand lessons and instructions written in English;
2. read textbooks, news, stories and reference materials;
3. explain your ideas in essays, letters, reports and discussions;
4. understand spoken information and respond appropriately; and
5. prepare for examinations and further study in which English is used.

These skills grow through practice. A learner who reads regularly, asks about unfamiliar words, reviews corrections and writes often has more opportunities to improve than a learner who studies only shortly before an examination.

### 1.3 What the examinations assess

WAEC and NECO assess candidates through several forms of English-language work. The exact paper arrangement, duration, instructions and marks can differ between examination bodies and may change. Always follow the current official syllabus, examination timetable and instructions issued for your examination series. Do not rely on an old cover, an online prediction or a timetable from another year.

In broad terms, prepare for these areas:

- **Lexis and structure:** choosing words and grammatical forms that fit a sentence or context.
- **Continuous writing:** writing an appropriate letter, speech, article, narrative, description, argument or other requested text.
- **Comprehension:** understanding stated information, vocabulary in context, relationships between ideas, and reasonable inferences from a passage.
- **Summary:** selecting the relevant points and expressing them clearly and concisely according to the question's instructions.
- **Oral English:** recognizing contrasts in English sounds, syllables, stress and other pronunciation features included in the examination.

The chapters in this handbook develop these areas alongside grammar, concord, vocabulary, figures of speech, registers and examination practice. A topic may support more than one paper. For example, grammar and vocabulary affect both objective questions and the accuracy of an essay.

### 1.4 Find out what your examination requires

At the beginning of your preparation, write down whether you are sitting WAEC, NECO or both, and the examination series. Then:

1. obtain the current syllabus or official examination guidance for that series;
2. check the relevant timetable and paper instructions when they are released;
3. ask your English teacher to explain any paper codes or instructions you do not understand; and
4. use past questions as practice only when their examination body and year are clearly identified.

WAEC's e-learning portal publishes examination materials and examiner feedback for selected earlier English Language examinations. These can help you understand the standard expected, but an older paper does not guarantee the format or questions for a future sitting. NECO candidates should use NECO's own current notices and timetable as well. The official links are listed in the adult reference notes for this chapter.

### 1.5 Build a steady study routine

English improves when you practise its different skills over time. A simple weekly plan might include:

| Study session | Practice |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Review one grammar topic and answer a short set of questions. |
| 2 | Read a passage and answer comprehension questions using evidence from the text. |
| 3 | Write one paragraph, letter section or essay plan; revise it after feedback. |
| 4 | Learn and use new words; review confusing words and idioms in context. |
| 5 | Practise oral English by identifying sounds, syllables and stress patterns. |
| 6 | Complete a mixed practice set under a reasonable time limit and review every mistake. |

Adjust the plan to your school timetable and other responsibilities. Short, regular sessions are easier to review than one long session that leaves no time to correct errors. Keep a notebook with three columns: **question or error**, **correction**, and **what I will remember next time**. Review it each week.

### 1.6 Prepare effectively for examination day

Before the examination:

- revise from the syllabus and your own error notes rather than trying to memorise every page;
- practise following instructions and completing work within the stated time;
- sleep and prepare the permitted materials your school or examination authority specifies; and
- confirm the venue, reporting time and current examination instructions with your school.

During the examination:

1. Read the instructions on the question paper carefully.
2. Check how many questions you must answer and whether choices are provided.
3. Divide your time so that each required section receives attention; reserve a few minutes to review your work.
4. In writing tasks, identify the audience, purpose and required form before you begin. Make a brief plan, organize your paragraphs and leave time to check grammar, spelling and punctuation.
5. In comprehension and summary tasks, answer the question asked and use the passage as your evidence. Follow any word, sentence or point limits stated on the paper.
6. In objective questions, read all the options and select the one that best fits the context. If you are unsure, move on and return if time permits.
7. Write legibly and follow all examination rules. Do not use unauthorized materials or seek help during the examination.

No study guide can guarantee a particular grade. Your result depends on your preparation, the questions set and your performance on the day. The purpose of practice is to help you understand the skills, notice what needs work and approach the examination with greater confidence.

### Chapter review

- English study develops listening, speaking, reading and writing, supported by grammar and vocabulary.
- WAEC and NECO have their own official notices and examination arrangements. Check current instructions for your examination series.
- Regular practice and correction help you identify what to revise next.

Chapter Two

Grammar: Parts of Speech

Learning Objectives

At the end of this chapter, students should be able to:

- Define grammar and explain its importance.
- Identify the eight major parts of speech.
- Use each part of speech correctly in speech and writing.
- Recognise common grammatical errors.
- Answer WAEC and NECO grammar questions with confidence.

2.1 Meaning of Grammar

Grammar is the system of rules that governs the correct use of a language. It explains how words are formed and arranged into meaningful sentences. Good grammar promotes effective communication and is essential for success in WAEC and NECO English Language examinations.

2.2 The Parts of Speech

The parts of speech are the categories into which words are classified according to their functions in a sentence.

The major parts of speech are:

1. Noun
2. Pronoun
3. Verb
4. Adjective
5. Adverb
6. Preposition
7. Conjunction
8. Interjection

---

2.3 Nouns

A noun is the name of a person, place, animal, thing or idea.

Types of Nouns

(a) Proper Nouns

These are specific names and begin with capital letters.

Examples:

- Nigeria
- Lagos
- Benin City
- Amina
- River Niger

(b) Common Nouns

These refer to general names.

Examples:

- teacher
- boy
- market
- school
- river

(c) Concrete Nouns

Names of things that can be seen or touched.

Examples:

- table
- book
- dog
- chair

(d) Abstract Nouns

Names of qualities, ideas or feelings.

Examples:

- honesty
- courage
- wisdom
- happiness

(e) Collective Nouns

Names of groups.

Examples:

- team
- committee
- family
- jury
- crowd

---

2.4 Pronouns

A pronoun replaces a noun to avoid unnecessary repetition.

Examples:

- I
- you
- he
- she
- it
- we
- they

Types of Pronouns

- Personal Pronouns
- Possessive Pronouns
- Reflexive Pronouns
- Demonstrative Pronouns
- Relative Pronouns
- Interrogative Pronouns
- Indefinite Pronouns

Example:

John is a brilliant student. He studies every day.

---

2.5 Verbs

A verb expresses an action or a state of being.

Examples:

- run
- write
- eat
- sing
- is
- was
- become

Types of Verbs

- Action Verbs
- Linking Verbs
- Auxiliary (Helping) Verbs
- Modal Verbs

Example:

The students are writing the examination.

---

2.6 Adjectives

An adjective describes or qualifies a noun or pronoun.

Examples:

- beautiful girl
- intelligent student
- tall building
- blue shirt
- five books

Degrees of Comparison

Positive: tall

Comparative: taller

Superlative: tallest

Example:

James is taller than David.

John is the tallest student in the class.

---

2.7 Adverbs

An adverb modifies a verb, an adjective or another adverb.

Examples:

- quickly
- slowly
- carefully
- yesterday
- very
- quite

Example:

The teacher spoke clearly.

---

2.8 Prepositions

A preposition shows the relationship between a noun (or pronoun) and another word.

Common prepositions include:

- in
- on
- under
- above
- beside
- between
- among
- during
- across
- into

Examples:

The book is on the table.

She travelled to Abuja.

---

2.9 Conjunctions

A conjunction joins words, phrases or clauses.

Common conjunctions include:

- and
- but
- or
- because
- although
- since
- while
- if

Examples:

Peter and Paul are friends.

She studied hard because she wanted to pass.

---

2.10 Interjections

An interjection expresses sudden emotion.

Examples:

- Oh!
- Wow!
- Alas!
- Hurrah!
- Ouch!

Example:

Hurrah! We have won the competition.

---

2.11 Common WAEC/NECO Errors

Incorrect: She don't like rice.

Correct: She doesn't like rice.

Incorrect: The committee have agreed.

Correct: The committee has agreed.

Incorrect: He is more taller than James.

Correct: He is taller than James.

Incorrect: I am going in school.

Correct: I am going to school.

Incorrect: He did the work good.

Correct: He did the work well.

---

Examination Tips

- Identify the function of the underlined word before choosing your answer.
- Read the whole sentence carefully.
- Watch out for tense and subject-verb agreement.
- Eliminate obviously incorrect options first.
- Practise grammar exercises daily.

---

Chapter Summary

Grammar is the foundation of effective communication. A sound knowledge of the parts of speech enables students to construct correct sentences and perform well in the Lexis and Structure section of WAEC and NECO examinations.

Chapter Three

Tenses and Sentence Structure

Learning Objectives

At the end of this chapter, students should be able to:

- Explain the concept of tense.
- Identify and correctly use the twelve English tenses.
- Construct grammatically correct sentences.
- Distinguish between active and passive voice.
- Change direct speech to indirect speech and vice versa.
- Avoid common sentence errors tested in WAEC and NECO examinations.

---

3.1 Meaning of Tense

A tense is the form of a verb that shows the time an action takes place. The action may occur in the present, past, or future.

Correct use of tense is essential for speaking and writing accurate English.

---

3.2 The Twelve English Tenses

The English language has twelve major tenses.

A. Present Tenses

1. Simple Present Tense

Structure: Subject + Base Verb (+ s/es)

Examples

- She teaches English.
- They play football every Saturday.
- The sun rises in the east.

Uses

- Habitual actions.
- General truths.
- Permanent situations.

---

2. Present Continuous Tense

Structure: Subject + is/am/are + Verb + ing

Examples:

- I am reading.
- They are studying.
- She is cooking dinner.

Uses:

- Actions happening now.
- Temporary activities.

---

3. Present Perfect Tense

Structure: Subject + has/have + Past Participle

Examples:

- I have completed my assignment.
- She has travelled to Abuja.
- They have finished the work.

Uses:

- Actions completed recently.
- Actions whose effects remain.

---

4. Present Perfect Continuous

Structure: Subject + has/have been + Verb + ing

Examples:

- He has been reading for two hours.
- We have been waiting since morning.

---

B. Past Tenses

5. Simple Past Tense

Examples:

- She visited Benin yesterday.
- They arrived early.
- I wrote the examination last week.

---

6. Past Continuous Tense

Structure:

Subject + was/were + Verb + ing

Examples:

- They were playing football.
- She was sleeping when I arrived.

---

7. Past Perfect Tense

Structure:

Subject + had + Past Participle

Examples:

- The train had left before we arrived.
- She had completed the work.

---

8. Past Perfect Continuous

Structure:

Subject + had been + Verb + ing

Examples:

- He had been teaching for ten years.
- They had been waiting for several hours.

---

C. Future Tenses

9. Simple Future Tense

Structure:

Subject + shall/will + Base Verb

Examples:

- I will visit Lagos tomorrow.
- She will succeed.

---

10. Future Continuous

Structure:

Subject + will be + Verb + ing

Examples:

- We will be travelling tomorrow.
- She will be reading at this time.

---

11. Future Perfect

Structure:

Subject + will have + Past Participle

Examples:

- They will have completed the project.
- I will have finished the examination.

---

12. Future Perfect Continuous

Structure:

Subject + will have been + Verb + ing

Examples:

- She will have been teaching for twenty years next month.
- We will have been living here for ten years.

---

3.3 Sentence Structure

A sentence is a group of words that expresses a complete thought.

Every complete sentence has:

- Subject
- Predicate

Example:

The students passed the examination.

Subject: The students

Predicate: passed the examination.

---

3.4 Types of Sentences

Declarative Sentence

Makes a statement.

Example:

The examination begins at 9 a.m.

---

Interrogative Sentence

Asks a question.

Example:

Have you completed your assignment?

---

Imperative Sentence

Gives a command or instruction.

Example:

Close the door.

---

Exclamatory Sentence

Expresses strong emotion.

Example:

What a wonderful performance!

---

3.5 Active and Passive Voice

Active Voice

The subject performs the action.

Example:

The teacher marked the scripts.

Passive Voice

The subject receives the action.

Example:

The scripts were marked by the teacher.

More Examples

Active: The police arrested the suspect.

Passive: The suspect was arrested by the police.

Active: She wrote the letter.

Passive: The letter was written by her.

---

3.6 Direct and Indirect Speech

Direct Speech

The speaker's exact words are quoted.

Example:

Mary said, "I am happy."

Indirect Speech

The speaker's words are reported without quotation marks.

Example:

Mary said that she was happy.

Rules

- Change pronouns where necessary.
- Change tense appropriately.
- Remove quotation marks.
- Use reporting verbs correctly.

---

3.7 Common Sentence Errors

Incorrect: He don't understand.

Correct: He doesn't understand.

Incorrect: She have gone home.

Correct: She has gone home.

Incorrect: They was late.

Correct: They were late.

Incorrect: I am knowing him.

Correct: I know him.

Incorrect: He did not went.

Correct: He did not go.

---

3.8 Examination Tips

- Identify time expressions before choosing the tense.
- Read every sentence carefully.
- Ensure subject-verb agreement.
- Avoid mixing tenses unnecessarily.
- Practise changing active voice to passive voice regularly.
- Learn the rules governing reported speech.

---

Chapter Summary

Correct use of tense and sentence structure is fundamental to good English. Students who master the twelve tenses, sentence patterns, active and passive voice, and reported speech are well prepared for the Lexis and Structure section of WAEC and NECO examinations.

Chapter Four

Concord (Subject–Verb Agreement)

Learning Objectives

At the end of this chapter, students should be able to:

- Define concord.
- Explain the rules governing subject–verb agreement.
- Identify common concord errors.
- Apply concord rules correctly in speech and writing.
- Answer WAEC and NECO concord questions accurately.

---

4.1 Meaning of Concord

Concord, also known as subject–verb agreement, is the grammatical relationship in which the verb agrees with its subject in number and person.

A singular subject takes a singular verb, while a plural subject takes a plural verb.

Examples

- The boy plays football. ✓
- The boys play football. ✓

---

4.2 Basic Rules of Concord

Rule 1: Singular Subject Takes a Singular Verb

Examples:

- The teacher teaches English.
- My sister lives in Abuja.
- The dog barks loudly.

---

Rule 2: Plural Subject Takes a Plural Verb

Examples:

- The teachers teach English.
- The students study hard.
- The players train every morning.

---

Rule 3: Compound Subjects Joined by "And"

When two singular nouns are joined by and, they usually take a plural verb.

Examples:

- John and Mary are friends.
- Bread and butter are available.

Exception

If the two nouns refer to one person or one idea, use a singular verb.

Examples:

- Bread and butter is my favourite breakfast.
- Rice and stew is served every afternoon.

---

Rule 4: Subjects Joined by "Either...Or" and "Neither...Nor"

The verb agrees with the subject nearest to it.

Examples:

- Either the teacher or the students are coming.
- Either the students or the teacher is coming.
- Neither Musa nor James is present.
- Neither the boys nor their father is at home.

---

Rule 5: Indefinite Pronouns

Words such as everyone, everybody, someone, somebody, anyone, anybody, nobody, no one, each, either, neither take singular verbs.

Examples:

- Everyone is ready.
- Nobody knows the answer.
- Each of the players has a jersey.

---

Rule 6: Collective Nouns

Collective nouns generally take singular verbs when regarded as one unit.

Examples:

- The committee has submitted its report.
- The family is travelling tomorrow.
- The jury has reached a verdict.

---

Rule 7: Uncountable Nouns

Uncountable nouns always take singular verbs.

Examples:

- Information is useful.
- Furniture is expensive.
- Advice is valuable.
- Equipment has arrived.

---

Rule 8: Amounts of Money, Time and Distance

When regarded as one unit, they take singular verbs.

Examples:

- Five thousand naira is enough.
- Ten kilometres is a long distance.
- Two hours is sufficient.

---

Rule 9: Titles of Books, Films and Newspapers

Titles take singular verbs even when they appear plural.

Examples:

- Great Expectations is an interesting novel.
- The Chronicles of Narnia is widely read.

---

Rule 10: "One of..."

Use a singular verb after "one of".

Examples:

- One of the students has arrived.
- One of the teachers was absent.

---

Rule 11: "A Number of" and "The Number of"

- A number of students are absent.
- The number of students is increasing.

---

Rule 12: Words Ending in "-s" but Singular in Meaning

Examples:

- Mathematics is my favourite subject.
- Economics is interesting.
- Physics is difficult for some students.

---

4.3 Common Concord Errors

Incorrect: She don't know me.

Correct: She doesn't know me.

Incorrect: Everybody have arrived.

Correct: Everybody has arrived.

Incorrect: The committee have agreed.

Correct: The committee has agreed.

Incorrect: Neither the principal nor the teachers is coming.

Correct: Neither the principal nor the teachers are coming.

Incorrect: One of the boys are absent.

Correct: One of the boys is absent.

---

4.4 Tips for Answering Concord Questions

- Identify the main subject before choosing the verb.
- Ignore words between the subject and the verb.
- Be careful with collective nouns and indefinite pronouns.
- Watch out for expressions such as either...or, neither...nor, a number of, and the number of.
- Read each sentence carefully before selecting an answer.

---

Chapter Summary

Concord is one of the most frequently tested areas in WAEC and NECO English Language examinations. Students who master the rules of subject–verb agreement are less likely to make grammatical errors in essays, comprehension, summary writing and objective questions.

Chapter Five

Lexis and Structure

Learning Objectives

At the end of this chapter, students should be able to:

- Explain the meaning of lexis and structure.
- Use synonyms, antonyms, homonyms and homophones correctly.
- Recognise and use idiomatic expressions, phrasal verbs and collocations.
- Complete sentence-completion questions accurately.
- Answer WAEC and NECO Lexis and Structure questions with confidence.

---

5.1 Meaning of Lexis and Structure

Lexis refers to the vocabulary or stock of words in a language, while Structure refers to the grammatical arrangement of words into meaningful sentences.

The Lexis and Structure section of WAEC and NECO tests a candidate's knowledge of vocabulary, grammar, idiomatic usage and sentence construction.

---

5.2 Synonyms

A synonym is a word that has the same or nearly the same meaning as another word.

Word| Synonym
Abandon| Desert
Abundant| Plentiful
Accurate| Correct
Brave| Courageous
Calm| Peaceful
Diligent| Hardworking
Famous| Renowned
Honest| Truthful
Intelligent| Brilliant
Prosper| Flourish

Example

The principal praised the diligent student.

A word closest in meaning to diligent is hardworking.

---

5.3 Antonyms

An antonym is a word opposite in meaning to another word.

Word| Antonym
Ancient| Modern
Arrival| Departure
Beautiful| Ugly
Cheap| Expensive
Increase| Decrease
Maximum| Minimum
Success| Failure
Victory| Defeat
Wise| Foolish
Strong| Weak

---

5.4 Homonyms

Homonyms are words that have the same spelling or pronunciation but different meanings.

Examples:

- Bank (financial institution)

- Bank (side of a river)

- Bat (animal)

- Bat (used in cricket or baseball)

- Bark (sound made by a dog)

- Bark (outer covering of a tree)

---

5.5 Homophones

Homophones are words that sound alike but differ in spelling and meaning.

Examples:

Word| Homophone
Flour| Flower
Son| Sun
Right| Write
Sea| See
Sale| Sail
Weak| Week
Pair| Pear
Peace| Piece
Mail| Male
Allowed| Aloud

---

5.6 Idiomatic Expressions

Idioms are expressions whose meanings cannot be understood from the individual words.

Examples:

- Once in a blue moon – very rarely.
- Hit the nail on the head – to be exactly right.
- Spill the beans – reveal a secret.
- Break the ice – begin a conversation.
- At the eleventh hour – at the last moment.
- A blessing in disguise – something good that first appeared bad.
- Beat about the bush – avoid the main issue.
- Burn the midnight oil – study or work late into the night.
- Cost an arm and a leg – very expensive.
- Keep an eye on – watch carefully.

---

5.7 Phrasal Verbs

A phrasal verb consists of a verb and one or more particles (prepositions or adverbs).

Examples:

Phrasal Verb| Meaning
Bring up| Raise a child
Carry out| Perform
Call off| Cancel
Give up| Stop trying
Look after| Take care of
Look into| Investigate
Put off| Postpone
Set up| Establish
Turn down| Reject
Work out| Solve

---

5.8 Collocations

Collocations are words that naturally occur together.

Examples:

- Make a decision
- Make a mistake
- Pay attention
- Heavy rain
- Strong coffee
- Fast food
- Catch a bus
- Commit a crime
- Tell the truth
- Keep a promise

Incorrect collocations often attract penalties in examinations.

---

5.9 One-Word Substitutes

Instead of using many words, English often has a single word.

Examples:

Expression| One Word
One who cannot read or write| Illiterate
One who writes books| Author
One who flies an aircraft| Pilot
One who studies plants| Botanist
One who studies animals| Zoologist
One who treats diseases| Physician
One who cannot hear| Deaf
One who cannot speak| Dumb (historical usage; "mute" is preferred in modern English)
One who loves mankind| Philanthropist
One who loves wisdom| Philosopher

---

5.10 Sentence Completion

Choose the word that best completes the sentence.

Example 1

The chairman __________ the meeting before noon.

a. dissolved

b. concluded

c. abandoned

d. postponed

Correct Answer: b. concluded

Example 2

The principal advised the students to work __________.

a. hardly

b. hard

c. hardestly

d. harderly

Correct Answer: b. hard

---

5.11 Common Lexical Errors

Incorrect: He discussed about the issue.

Correct: He discussed the issue.

Incorrect: She requested for permission.

Correct: She requested permission.

Incorrect: I returned back yesterday.

Correct: I returned yesterday.

Incorrect: They emphasised on the point.

Correct: They emphasised the point.

Incorrect: The teacher comprised of five members.

Correct: The committee comprised five members.

---

Examination Tips

- Read the entire sentence before selecting an answer.
- Use context clues to determine the correct meaning.
- Learn new words every day.
- Study idiomatic expressions and collocations regularly.
- Practise past WAEC and NECO objective questions consistently.

Chapter Six

Comprehension

Learning Objectives

At the end of this chapter, students should be able to:

- Explain the meaning and purpose of comprehension.

- Identify the main ideas and supporting details in a passage.
- Infer the meanings of unfamiliar words from context.

---

6.1 Meaning of Comprehension

Comprehension is the ability to read, understand, interpret and evaluate a written passage. It goes beyond merely recognising words; it involves understanding the writer's message and responding correctly to questions based on the passage.

In WAEC and NECO examinations, comprehension tests a candidate's ability to:

- Understand facts and ideas.
- Draw logical conclusions.
- Interpret vocabulary in context.
- Identify the writer's purpose and attitude.
- Summarise information accurately.

---

6.2 Types of Comprehension Questions

Candidates may encounter the following types of questions:

(a) Factual Questions

These require answers directly stated in the passage.

Example

Where did the writer spend his holiday?

---

(b) Inferential Questions

These require candidates to draw conclusions from information provided.

Example

Why was the principal disappointed with the students?

---

(c) Vocabulary in Context

Candidates explain the meaning of a word or expression as used in the passage.

Example

What does the word "resilient" mean as used in the passage?

---

(d) Writer's Attitude or Tone

These questions ask candidates to identify the writer's feelings or opinions.

Examples include:

- optimistic
- pessimistic
- humorous
- sympathetic
- critical
- objective

---

6.3 Reading Techniques

Successful candidates usually follow these steps:

Skimming

Read quickly to obtain the general idea of the passage.

Scanning

Read carefully to locate specific information.

Intensive Reading

Study the passage in detail to understand important points.

Critical Reading

Analyse the writer's arguments before answering questions.

---

6.4 Steps for Answering Comprehension Questions

1. Read the title carefully.
2. Read the entire passage once.
3. Read the questions before reading the passage again.
4. Underline important words and ideas.
5. Answer only from information contained in the passage.
6. Use your own words where required.
7. Avoid copying long sections unnecessarily.
8. Review your answers before moving to the next section.

---

6.5 Vocabulary in Context

The meaning of a word depends on how it is used.

Example

"The farmer was diligent, working from sunrise until sunset."

Here, diligent means hardworking.

Another Example

"The governor's decision was controversial."

Here, controversial means causing disagreement or public debate.

4. He received a university scholarship.

---

6.7 Common Mistakes Made by Candidates

- Reading too quickly.
- Ignoring important details.
- Introducing personal opinions not found in the passage.
- Copying entire paragraphs instead of answering precisely.
- Misinterpreting vocabulary.
- Poor time management.

---

6.8 Examination Tips

- Read every passage at least twice.
- Pay attention to signal words such as however, therefore, although and because.
- Use complete but concise answers.
- Where instructed, answer in your own words.
- Check your grammar and spelling before submitting your script.

Chapter Seven

Summary Writing

Learning Objectives

At the end of this chapter, students should be able to:

---

7.1 Meaning of Summary Writing

Summary writing is the process of presenting the main ideas of a passage in a brief, clear and accurate form without changing the original meaning.

A good summary contains only the essential points and excludes examples, illustrations, repetitions and unnecessary details.

---

7.2 Importance of Summary Writing

Summary writing helps students to:

- Identify important information.
- Improve reading and comprehension skills.
- Develop clear and concise writing.
- Organise ideas logically.
- Prepare effectively for WAEC and NECO examinations.
- Communicate information accurately.

---

7.3 Features of a Good Summary

A good summary should:

- Be brief and precise.
- Contain only the main ideas.
- Be written in the candidate's own words where required.
- Preserve the original meaning.
- Follow a logical order.
- Be grammatically correct.
- Avoid unnecessary repetition.

---

7.4 Steps in Writing a Good Summary

Step 1: Read the Passage Carefully

Read the passage at least twice to understand the central message.

Step 2: Identify the Main Ideas

Underline or note the key points in each paragraph.

Step 3: Eliminate Minor Details

Ignore illustrations, anecdotes, examples and repeated ideas.

Step 4: Rewrite the Main Ideas

Express the ideas briefly using your own words whenever instructed.

Step 5: Revise Your Work

Check for grammar, spelling, punctuation and completeness.

---

7.5 Main Ideas and Supporting Details

Main Idea

The central message of a paragraph.

Supporting Details

Facts, examples or explanations that develop the main idea.

Example

Paragraph:

"Regular exercise improves physical health, strengthens the heart, reduces stress and increases energy."

Main Idea:

Regular exercise improves health.

Supporting Details:

- Strengthens the heart.
- Reduces stress.
- Increases energy.

---

7.6 Common Errors in Summary Writing

Candidates often:

- Copy long sentences directly from the passage.
- Include unnecessary examples.
- Introduce personal opinions.
- Omit important points.
- Write excessively long answers.
- Ignore the examiner's instructions.

---

2. In one sentence, state what successful students avoid.

---

Suggested Answers

1. Successful students organise their studies through careful planning, regular attendance, prompt completion of assignments and consistent revision.

2. Successful students avoid distractions such as excessive television viewing and unnecessary use of social media.

---

7.8 Examination Techniques

To perform well:

- Read every question before reading the passage again.
- Note the number of points required.
- Answer only what is asked.
- Use simple and correct English.
- Write complete sentences unless instructed otherwise.
- Observe any specified word limit.
- Revise your answers before submitting your script.

---

7.9 Difference Between Comprehension and Summary

Comprehension| Summary
Tests understanding of a passage| Tests ability to condense information
May require detailed answers| Requires brief answers
Includes vocabulary questions| Focuses on main ideas
May include inference| Emphasises concise expression

Chapter Eight

Essay Writing

Learning Objectives

At the end of this chapter, students should be able to:

- Explain the meaning and importance of essay writing.
- Identify the different types of essays tested in WAEC and NECO.
- Organise ideas into clear paragraphs.
- Write effective introductions, body paragraphs and conclusions.
- Apply the principles of good English in essay writing.
- Produce well-developed essays capable of earning high marks.

---

8.1 Meaning of Essay Writing

An essay is a piece of continuous writing in which a writer presents ideas, facts, opinions or experiences on a given topic in a logical and organised manner.

Essay writing enables candidates to demonstrate their command of grammar, vocabulary, organisation of ideas and creativity.

---

8.2 Importance of Essay Writing

Essay writing helps students to:

- Develop effective communication skills.
- Express ideas clearly and logically.
- Improve grammar and vocabulary.
- Develop critical and creative thinking.
- Prepare for school and public examinations.
- Enhance confidence in written communication.

---

8.3 Features of a Good Essay

A good essay should have:

- A suitable title.
- A clear introduction.
- Well-organised body paragraphs.
- Logical presentation of ideas.
- Correct grammar and punctuation.
- Appropriate vocabulary.
- Smooth transitions between paragraphs.
- A strong conclusion.

---

8.4 Stages in Essay Writing

Stage One: Understand the Topic

Read the question carefully and identify exactly what is required.

Stage Two: Brainstorm

Write down relevant ideas before you begin.

Stage Three: Prepare an Outline

Arrange your ideas in logical order.

Stage Four: Write the First Draft

Develop your introduction, body and conclusion.

Stage Five: Revise

Correct grammatical errors, spelling mistakes and punctuation before submitting your work.

---

8.5 Types of Essays

(A) Narrative Essay

A narrative essay tells a story or recounts an event.

Examples:

- An unforgettable day in my life.
- How I spent my last holiday.
- A journey I will never forget.

Guidelines

- Present events in chronological order.
- Use mainly the past tense.
- Include characters, setting and sequence of events.
- End with a meaningful conclusion.

---

(B) Descriptive Essay

A descriptive essay gives a vivid picture of a person, place, object or event.

Examples:

- My School.
- A Market Scene.
- My Favourite Teacher.

Guidelines

- Appeal to the reader's senses.
- Use descriptive adjectives.
- Present details logically.

---

(C) Expository Essay

An expository essay explains or informs.

Examples:

- The Importance of Education.
- Causes of Youth Unemployment.
- Advantages of Modern Technology.

Guidelines

- Explain clearly.
- Support ideas with relevant facts.
- Avoid unnecessary personal opinions.

---

(D) Argumentative Essay

An argumentative essay presents arguments for and against an issue before reaching a logical conclusion.

Examples:

- Should School Uniforms Be Compulsory?
- Social Media Does More Harm Than Good.
- Democracy Is Better Than Military Rule.

Guidelines

- Present both sides fairly.
- Support arguments with evidence and examples.
- End with a convincing conclusion.

---

(E) Article Writing

An article is written for publication in a newspaper, magazine or school magazine.

Features include:

- Attractive title.
- Name of writer where required.
- Clear introduction.
- Informative body.
- Logical conclusion.

---

(F) Speech Writing

A speech is prepared to be delivered before an audience.

Features include:

- Appropriate salutation.
- Introduction of the topic.
- Clear body.
- Conclusion with appreciation.

Example opening:

The Chairman, distinguished guests, teachers and fellow students, good morning.

---

8.6 Paragraph Development

Each paragraph should contain:

- A topic sentence.
- Supporting ideas.
- Relevant examples where necessary.
- A concluding sentence.

One paragraph should normally contain one major idea.

---

8.7 Common Errors in Essay Writing

Avoid:

- Poor handwriting.
- Grammatical errors.
- Wrong spellings.
- Slang and informal expressions.
- Repetition of ideas.
- Poor paragraphing.
- Writing off the topic.
- Failure to revise your work.

---

8.8 Examination Tips

- Choose the essay topic you understand best.
- Spend a few minutes preparing an outline.
- Allocate your time wisely.
- Write legibly.
- Use correct grammar and punctuation.
- Support your ideas with relevant examples.
- Leave a few minutes to revise your work.

---

- Meaning of education.
- Importance to individuals and society.

Body

- Reduces poverty.
- Creates employment opportunities.
- Promotes national development.
- Encourages responsible citizenship.

Conclusion

Chapter Eleven

Figures of Speech and Literary Devices

Learning Objectives

At the end of this chapter, students should be able to:

- Distinguish between different literary devices.
- Use figures of speech appropriately in speaking and writing.
- Answer WAEC and NECO questions on figures of speech correctly.

---

11.1 Meaning of Figures of Speech

A figure of speech is a word or expression used in a non-literal or imaginative way to make communication more vivid, interesting and effective.

Writers and speakers use figures of speech to create emphasis, beauty and deeper meaning.

---

11.2 Simile

A simile compares two different things using the words "like" or "as."

Examples

- She is as brave as a lion.
- He runs like the wind.
- The baby slept like a log.

---

11.3 Metaphor

A metaphor compares two unlike things without using "like" or "as."

Examples

- Time is money.
- The classroom was a zoo.
- Her voice is music to my ears.

---

11.4 Personification

Personification gives human qualities to animals, objects or ideas.

Examples

- The wind whispered through the trees.
- The sun smiled at us.
- Opportunity knocked on the door.

---

11.5 Hyperbole

Hyperbole is deliberate exaggeration for emphasis.

Examples

- I have told you a thousand times.
- The bag weighs a ton.
- She cried a river.

---

11.6 Irony

Irony occurs when the opposite of what is expected happens, or when words convey the opposite of their literal meaning.

Examples

- A fire station burns down.
- A traffic officer arrives late because of traffic.
- Saying, "What lovely weather!" during a heavy storm.

---

11.7 Euphemism

A euphemism is a mild or polite expression used instead of one that may sound harsh or unpleasant.

Examples

- "Passed away" instead of "died."
- "Senior citizen" instead of "old person."
- "Visually impaired" instead of "blind."

---

11.8 Oxymoron

An oxymoron combines two words with opposite meanings.

Examples

- Deafening silence
- Bitter sweet
- Living dead
- Open secret

---

11.9 Paradox

A paradox is a statement that appears contradictory but contains an underlying truth.

Examples

- Less is more.
- The only constant is change.
- To preserve peace, we must prepare for conflict.

---

11.10 Alliteration

Alliteration is the repetition of the same consonant sound at the beginning of nearby words.

Examples

- Peter Piper picked a peck of pickled peppers.
- Busy bees buzzed.
- Safe and sound.

---

11.11 Assonance

Assonance is the repetition of similar vowel sounds in nearby words.

Examples

- Go slow over the road.
- The rain in Spain falls mainly on the plain.

---

11.12 Onomatopoeia

Onomatopoeia is the use of words that imitate natural sounds.

Examples

- Buzz
- Bang
- Splash
- Tick-tock
- Hiss
- Roar

---

11.13 Pun

A pun is a play on words in which one word has two meanings or two similar-sounding words are used for humorous effect.

Example

"The fisherman was hooked on his job."

---

11.14 Litotes

Litotes expresses an idea by denying its opposite, usually for emphasis.

Examples

- He is not a bad student.
- The examination was not impossible.

---

11.15 Common WAEC/NECO Questions

Candidates may be asked to:

- Identify the figure of speech used in a sentence.
- Explain the meaning of an expression.
- Distinguish between similar literary devices.
- Use a figure of speech correctly in a sentence.

---

11.16 Common Errors

Students often confuse:

- Simile and metaphor.
- Hyperbole and irony.
- Oxymoron and paradox.
- Alliteration and assonance.

Remember:

- Simile uses like or as.
- Metaphor does not.

---

Examination Tips

- Learn the definitions of common figures of speech.
- Study several examples of each device.
- Read novels, poems and newspapers to observe figurative language in context.
- Practise identifying literary devices in sentences.

---

Figures of speech enrich language by making communication more expressive and engaging. Mastery of these devices improves students' understanding of comprehension passages, enhances essay writing and prepares them for WAEC and NECO English Language examinations.

Students preparing for an English examination should begin by checking the syllabus and listing the topics they need to study. The librarian organized a short workshop. She showed the class how to use the catalogue, compare information from more than one source and write down the title, author and date of a source while taking notes. The class then divided the project into questions: how water becomes contaminated, how communities can protect local sources, and where people can seek reliable public-health advice. ### Part D: Summary — 10 marks

In **four concise points**, state the advice the passage gives for checking and using information responsibly. Do not copy whole sentences.

Information is easier to use responsibly when a student first turns a broad topic into clear questions. The student should search for information that answers those questions rather than collect facts without a purpose. When using a book or web page, the student should note its author or organization, title and date. A web source should be checked for who produced it, when it was updated and whether another dependable source supports it. Important claims should be compared across sources because one page may contain errors or outdated information. While taking notes, students should distinguish the source's exact wording from their own explanation. They should not copy paragraphs and present them as their own work. Instead, they should select relevant points, explain them clearly in their own words and acknowledge the sources used. Before submitting a project, they should review whether each paragraph answers the topic and whether borrowed ideas have been credited.

16.1 Why Students Fail English Language

Many students fail not because they lack ability but because of poor preparation and ineffective examination techniques.

Common causes include:

- Poor reading culture.
- Weak vocabulary.
- Poor knowledge of grammar.
- Inadequate practice in essay writing.
- Failure to understand examination questions.
- Poor handwriting.
- Poor time management.
- Examination anxiety.
- Failure to revise regularly.

---

Successful candidates usually:

- Read English materials every day.
- Learn new words regularly.
- Speak correct English whenever possible.
- Practise essay and letter writing weekly.
- Revise grammar consistently.
- Solve past and model examination questions.
- Learn from previous mistakes.

---

16.3 Developing a Reading Culture

Students should cultivate the habit of reading:

- Newspapers.
- Magazines.
- Novels.
- Biographies.
- Academic textbooks.
- Educational websites.
- Dictionaries.

Benefits include:

- Better vocabulary.
- Improved comprehension.
- Better spelling.
- Better writing skills.
- Increased confidence.

16.4 Effective Communication Skills

Good communication involves:

- Listening attentively.
- Speaking clearly.
- Reading critically.
- Writing logically.
- Using appropriate vocabulary.
- Respecting the audience.

Paper duration and section timing vary by examination body and series. Read the instructions on your question paper, divide the available time among the required tasks, and leave a short period to review your answers. Use a timepiece only where the examination rules permit it.

Examiners award marks for correct answers—not for beautiful handwriting—but handwriting must be clear enough to be read.

Write:

- Neatly.
- Clearly.
- With adequate spacing.
- Without unnecessary cancellations.

Confidence comes from preparation.

- Study regularly.
- Revise difficult topics.
- Practise mock examinations.
- Believe in your ability.

- Avoid examination malpractice.
- Obey all examination rules.
