# Chapter One

## Introduction to English Language

### Learning objectives

By the end of this chapter, you should be able to:

- explain why English Language is an important school subject and communication tool;
- identify the broad skills assessed in senior secondary English examinations;
- describe the main areas you need to revise;
- plan regular, balanced preparation; and
- use practical strategies before and during an examination.

### 1.1 English as a language for communication

Language helps people share information, ask questions, explain ideas, tell stories and work together. English is used in many Nigerian classrooms, books, public services and workplaces. It is also a subject in the WAEC and NECO Senior School Certificate Examinations.

Learning English does not require you to abandon Nigerian languages or cultures. People can use more than one language, and each language can be valuable. This handbook focuses on the English skills required for school, examinations and wider communication.

Good communication involves more than memorising grammar rules. It includes listening carefully, speaking clearly, reading with understanding and writing in a way that suits the purpose and audience. Grammar, vocabulary and pronunciation help you use those skills accurately.

### 1.2 Why English Language matters

English can help you to:

1. understand lessons and instructions written in English;
2. read textbooks, news, stories and reference materials;
3. explain your ideas in essays, letters, reports and discussions;
4. understand spoken information and respond appropriately; and
5. prepare for examinations and further study in which English is used.

These skills grow through practice. A learner who reads regularly, asks about unfamiliar words, reviews corrections and writes often has more opportunities to improve than a learner who studies only shortly before an examination.

### 1.3 What the examinations assess

WAEC and NECO assess candidates through several forms of English-language work. The exact paper arrangement, duration, instructions and marks can differ between examination bodies and may change. Always follow the current official syllabus, examination timetable and instructions issued for your examination series. Do not rely on an old cover, an online prediction or a timetable from another year.

In broad terms, prepare for these areas:

- **Lexis and structure:** choosing words and grammatical forms that fit a sentence or context.
- **Continuous writing:** writing an appropriate letter, speech, article, narrative, description, argument or other requested text.
- **Comprehension:** understanding stated information, vocabulary in context, relationships between ideas, and reasonable inferences from a passage.
- **Summary:** selecting the relevant points and expressing them clearly and concisely according to the question's instructions.
- **Oral English:** recognizing contrasts in English sounds, syllables, stress and other pronunciation features included in the examination.

The chapters in this handbook develop these areas alongside grammar, concord, vocabulary, figures of speech, registers and examination practice. A topic may support more than one paper. For example, grammar and vocabulary affect both objective questions and the accuracy of an essay.

### 1.4 Find out what your examination requires

At the beginning of your preparation, write down whether you are sitting WAEC, NECO or both, and the examination series. Then:

1. obtain the current syllabus or official examination guidance for that series;
2. check the relevant timetable and paper instructions when they are released;
3. ask your English teacher to explain any paper codes or instructions you do not understand; and
4. use past questions as practice only when their examination body and year are clearly identified.

WAEC's e-learning portal publishes examination materials and examiner feedback for selected earlier English Language examinations. These can help you understand the standard expected, but an older paper does not guarantee the format or questions for a future sitting. NECO candidates should use NECO's own current notices and timetable as well. The official links are listed in the adult reference notes for this chapter.

### 1.5 Build a steady study routine

English improves when you practise its different skills over time. A simple weekly plan might include:

| Study session | Practice |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Review one grammar topic and answer a short set of questions. |
| 2 | Read a passage and answer comprehension questions using evidence from the text. |
| 3 | Write one paragraph, letter section or essay plan; revise it after feedback. |
| 4 | Learn and use new words; review confusing words and idioms in context. |
| 5 | Practise oral English by identifying sounds, syllables and stress patterns. |
| 6 | Complete a mixed practice set under a reasonable time limit and review every mistake. |

Adjust the plan to your school timetable and other responsibilities. Short, regular sessions are easier to review than one long session that leaves no time to correct errors. Keep a notebook with three columns: **question or error**, **correction**, and **what I will remember next time**. Review it each week.

### 1.6 Prepare effectively for examination day

Before the examination:

- revise from the syllabus and your own error notes rather than trying to memorise every page;
- practise following instructions and completing work within the stated time;
- sleep and prepare the permitted materials your school or examination authority specifies; and
- confirm the venue, reporting time and current examination instructions with your school.

During the examination:

1. Read the instructions on the question paper carefully.
2. Check how many questions you must answer and whether choices are provided.
3. Divide your time so that each required section receives attention; reserve a few minutes to review your work.
4. In writing tasks, identify the audience, purpose and required form before you begin. Make a brief plan, organize your paragraphs and leave time to check grammar, spelling and punctuation.
5. In comprehension and summary tasks, answer the question asked and use the passage as your evidence. Follow any word, sentence or point limits stated on the paper.
6. In objective questions, read all the options and select the one that best fits the context. If you are unsure, move on and return if time permits.
7. Write legibly and follow all examination rules. Do not use unauthorized materials or seek help during the examination.

No study guide can guarantee a particular grade. Your result depends on your preparation, the questions set and your performance on the day. The purpose of practice is to help you understand the skills, notice what needs work and approach the examination with greater confidence.

### Chapter review

- English study develops listening, speaking, reading and writing, supported by grammar and vocabulary.
- WAEC and NECO have their own official notices and examination arrangements. Check current instructions for your examination series.
- Regular practice and correction help you identify what to revise next.
- Read every examination instruction carefully, manage your time and answer the task that is set.
