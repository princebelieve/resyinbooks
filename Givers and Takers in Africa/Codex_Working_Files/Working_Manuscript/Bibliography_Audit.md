# Bibliography audit

This is a source review for the working manuscript, not a replacement bibliography. The supplied references are retained except for the verified Afrobarometer citation added to support a specific claim in Chapter One.

## Verified and used

- **Afrobarometer, 6 May 2025.** The release reports that 80% of surveyed Nigerians said corruption increased somewhat or a lot over the preceding year and describes citizens' fear of retaliation when reporting corruption. This supports the Chapter One summary. [Official release](https://www.afrobarometer.org/articles/nigerians-say-corruption-is-worsening-and-citizens-risk-retaliation-if-they-speak-up-afrobarometer-survey-finds/).
- **Ake, Claude, *Democracy and Development in Africa*.** Brookings lists a 1996 edition from Brookings Institution Press. The supplied 1996 entry is consistent with that record. The separate 2001 Spectrum Books entry needs the exact edition or ISBN confirmed before it is retained as a separate edition. [Brookings book record](https://www.brookings.edu/books/democracy-and-development-in-africa/).

## Repair before publication

- The source bibliography repeats Sen's *Development as Freedom* (1999) and North's *Institutions, Institutional Change and Economic Performance* (1990). Exact duplicate entries are removed once from the editable copy; the raw source preserves both occurrences.
- Several entries are placeholders rather than usable references: "Various years" for World Bank, IMF, UNDP and ILO publications; "Selected publications" on African traditional values; and a general Claude Ake Foundation publications entry.
- The proposed theoretical foundations name Adam Smith, Emile Durkheim, Max Weber, Abraham Maslow, Peter Drucker, Robert Putnam, Amartya Sen, Desmond Tutu and John Rawls. Several are not represented by complete entries in the supplied bibliography. The brief itself is not manuscript text, but it identifies intended intellectual foundations that may need references if those ideas remain in the chapters.
- Most chapters do not currently contain in-text citations that connect claims to the selected bibliography. The Afrobarometer citation is the exception added in this first assembly. Specific empirical and historical claims need source-by-source review.
- The references should be checked against the editions Prof used. Where edition details cannot be confirmed, leave a query for Prof rather than guessing.
